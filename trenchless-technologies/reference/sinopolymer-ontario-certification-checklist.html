Sino Polymer — Ontario CIPP Certification Checklist

What their resin line would actually need to clear to supply OPSS.MUNI 443/444/445 work — and what's still open.

The headline finding: every Sino Polymer product in the brochure numerically clears Ontario's generic baseline material minimums (OPSS.MUNI 443/444/445, 1,724 MPa / 31 MPa), on the neat-resin datasheet. That's necessary and nowhere near sufficient — Ontario's actual bar is installed-liner test data against the specific job's design values, not a resin spec sheet. This checklist separates what's already demonstrated from what's still an open item.
Update — the generic floor isn't the only floor, and it isn't the real one to check against. The Regional Municipality of Peel's own bid specifications (Lessons 26–28) set materials minimums well above the generic OPSS.MUNI figures: 2,400 MPa flexural modulus for non-reinforced liners (39% above the generic 1,724 MPa), and 8,000 MPa for reinforced/UV liners — more than 4.6x the generic floor. Sino Polymer's neat-resin data (2,900–4,100 MPa) still clears the non-reinforced bar, but says essentially nothing about whether a fiberglass-reinforced composite using their resin would clear 8,000 MPa — that's a composite-system number, not a resin number. Treat every municipality's own spec as potentially setting a higher bar than the generic Ontario floor, and check each one directly rather than assuming OPSS.MUNI's minimums are the ceiling. Section 1a below replaces the generic comparison with Peel's real numbers.

3. Watermain-only requirement — the real gap (445)

NSF/ANSI 61 certification is mandatory for every contact material on a 445 job — not optional, not "nice to have." The Sino Polymer brochure states only that "some products have passed NSF 61/NSF 51 certification" without naming which. Per Lesson 11's original flag, this has to be resolved to a specific certificate for a specific product before it can appear in a watermain bid. The two most likely candidates based on their drinking-water positioning are MFE 701-01T and MERICAN 9515N-01 — but "likely" isn't a submittable answer.

4. What Ontario does not require from the resin itself

Worth stating plainly, since it's easy to over-scope this checklist:

  • CSA PLUS 4012 is a condition-assessment/inspection protocol (Lesson 18, Lesson 21) — it governs how the existing pipe gets graded before a method is chosen, not a certification a resin or liner product needs to hold.
  • No separate Canadian resin certification scheme was found distinct from NSF/ANSI 61 (Lesson 13) — Canada relies on the same certification the US uses, not a parallel Canadian-specific standard.
  • BABA (Build America, Buy America) is a US federal requirement (Lesson 12) and does not apply to Ontario/Canadian municipal work — no domestic-sourcing constraint equivalent has been identified for Ontario specifically.

5. The gap that mattered most: neat resin vs. installed liner

The original brochure's numbers were all neat-resin data (ASTM D638/D790, cast resin alone). Ontario's OPSS.MUNI specs test the installed, cured composite liner — restrained cylindrical or flat-plate samples, tested by an independent third-party lab, compared against the project's design values (usually higher than the code floor). Generic OPSS.MUNI 443 (Lesson 23, §443.08.03.02) applies a single flat 15% de-rating to flat-plate results, full stop. Peel Region's own spec is more granular — its worked reconciliation example (Appendix 5.9, Lesson 28) applies two different de-rating figures to the same flat-plate sample: 15% for flexural modulus, 18% for flexural strength.
Update (Lessons 42–43): this is no longer the largest fully-open item — it's now a narrower confirmation task. Real cured-composite hose data (16,000–19,800 MPa flexural modulus) and real third-party long-term creep data (~43% retention) both now exist. Neither was tested to the exact named standard (ASTM D790/ISO 11296, ASTM D2990) Peel's spec cites, and neither was run at a specific contractor's exact job-site resin/fiber ratio — those two gaps are real and still open. But "does a composite made from this resin even get close to the bar" is answered, and answered well. Treat this section's remaining gap as "get the existing strong evidence formally re-tested to the named standard," not "start from nothing."

Quick checklist

  • ☑ Third-party creep-rupture test report, per product — exists for MERICAN 9405T (ISO 10468, Lesson 42). ☐ Confirm ASTM D2990 equivalence, or request a fresh D2990 test, before relying on it where D2990 is named specifically.
  • ☐ NSF/ANSI 61 certificate, per product, if targeting watermain (445) work — still genuinely open; the source document itself says NSF 61 is still being "actively pushed," not obtained (Lesson 44)
  • ☑ Chemical/abrasion/bacteriological resistance test reports — TÜV Rheinland (mechanics/corrosion) and UL 1316/1746 (corrosion, adjacent application) now exist (Lessons 43–44); not a direct F1216/F1743/F2019-cited test report
  • ☐ Resistance data against actual water treatment chemicals, for watermain work — still open
  • ☐ Joint-deflection installation and long-term survival data, for watermain work — still open
  • ☑ Composite-liner (not neat-resin) flexural/tensile test data, at realistic job fiber/resin ratios — real cured UV-CIPP hose composite data now exists (Lesson 43), 16,000–19,800 MPa flexural modulus; ☐ confirm at a specific contractor's exact job construction
  • ☐ Confirm the MERICAN 9516N-01 flexural modulus figure (8.3 GPa) with Sino Polymer before citing it anywhere (Lesson 11) — likely a transcription error
  • ☐ Tube traceability marking practice, confirmed directly
  • ☑ A resin reference sample process in place for per-project infrared spectrograph verification — in-house FTIR confirmed as part of Sino Polymer's own lab equipment (Lesson 45); process still needs confirming per-project
  • ☑ Composite-liner (fiberglass-reinforced) test data specifically against Peel Region's 8,000 MPa / 150 MPa reinforced minimums — real composite data clears both bars with real margin (Lesson 43); ☐ still needs testing to the named ASTM D790/ISO 11296 method specifically
  • ☐ Confirm which de-rating figure applies before treating a plate-sample result as passing: generic OPSS.MUNI 443 uses one flat 15%; Peel Region's own worked example (Lesson 28) splits this into 15% (modulus) and 18% (strength) — don't apply the wrong one
  • ☐ Ontario/North American CIPP installation history specifically — real project history exists (three large Shanghai-area jobs, Lesson 45) but none of it is regionally relevant to Peel's Ontario-experience requirement (Lesson 26)
