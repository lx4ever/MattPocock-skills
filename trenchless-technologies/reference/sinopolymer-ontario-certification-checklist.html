Sino Polymer — Ontario CIPP Certification Checklist

What their resin line would actually need to clear to supply OPSS.MUNI 443/444/445 work — and what's still open.

The headline finding: every Sino Polymer product in the brochure numerically clears Ontario's generic baseline material minimums (OPSS.MUNI 443/444/445, 1,724 MPa / 31 MPa), on the neat-resin datasheet. That's necessary and nowhere near sufficient — Ontario's actual bar is installed-liner test data against the specific job's design values, not a resin spec sheet. This checklist separates what's already demonstrated from what's still an open item.
Update — the generic floor isn't the only floor. The Regional Municipality of Peel's own bid specifications (Lessons 26–28) set materials minimums well above the generic OPSS.MUNI figures: 2,400 MPa flexural modulus for non-reinforced liners (39% above the generic 1,724 MPa), and 8,000 MPa for reinforced/UV liners — more than 4.6x the generic floor. Sino Polymer's neat-resin data (2,900–4,100 MPa) still clears the non-reinforced bar, but says essentially nothing about whether a fiberglass-reinforced composite using their resin would clear 8,000 MPa — that's a composite-system number, not a resin number. Treat every municipality's own spec as potentially setting a higher bar than the generic Ontario floor, and check each one directly rather than assuming OPSS.MUNI's minimums are the ceiling.

3. Watermain-only requirement — the real gap (445)

NSF/ANSI 61 certification is mandatory for every contact material on a 445 job — not optional, not "nice to have." The Sino Polymer brochure states only that "some products have passed NSF 61/NSF 51 certification" without naming which. Per Lesson 11's original flag, this has to be resolved to a specific certificate for a specific product before it can appear in a watermain bid. The two most likely candidates based on their drinking-water positioning are MFE 701-01T and MERICAN 9515N-01 — but "likely" isn't a submittable answer.

4. What Ontario does not require from the resin itself

Worth stating plainly, since it's easy to over-scope this checklist:

  • CSA PLUS 4012 is a condition-assessment/inspection protocol (Lesson 18, Lesson 21) — it governs how the existing pipe gets graded before a method is chosen, not a certification a resin or liner product needs to hold.
  • No separate Canadian resin certification scheme was found distinct from NSF/ANSI 61 (Lesson 13) — Canada relies on the same certification the US uses, not a parallel Canadian-specific standard.
  • BABA (Build America, Buy America) is a US federal requirement (Lesson 12) and does not apply to Ontario/Canadian municipal work — no domestic-sourcing constraint equivalent has been identified for Ontario specifically.

5. The gap that matters most: neat resin vs. installed liner

Every number in the brochure is neat-resin data (ASTM D638/D790, cast resin alone). Ontario's OPSS.MUNI specs test the installed, cured composite liner — restrained cylindrical or flat-plate samples, tested by an independent third-party lab, compared against the project's design values (usually higher than the code floor), with a 15% de-rating applied to flat-plate results. A resin that clears 1,724 MPa on its own datasheet by a wide margin says very little about whether a specific felt-or-fiberglass composite, at a specific job's resin/fiber ratio, will clear that same bar once cured in a real pipe. This is the single largest open item on the whole checklist — everything else is a paperwork gap; this is a testing gap.

Quick checklist

  • ☐ Third-party ASTM D2990 creep-rupture test report, per product
  • ☐ NSF/ANSI 61 certificate, per product, if targeting watermain (445) work
  • ☐ Chemical/abrasion/bacteriological resistance test reports (not just qualitative claims)
  • ☐ Resistance data against actual water treatment chemicals, for watermain work
  • ☐ Joint-deflection installation and long-term survival data, for watermain work
  • ☐ Composite-liner (not neat-resin) flexural/tensile test data, at realistic job fiber/resin ratios
  • ☐ Confirm the MERICAN 9516N-01 flexural modulus figure (8.3 GPa) with Sino Polymer before citing it anywhere (Lesson 11) — likely a transcription error
  • ☐ Tube traceability marking practice, confirmed directly
  • ☐ A resin reference sample process in place for per-project infrared spectrograph verification
  • ☐ Composite-liner (fiberglass-reinforced) test data specifically against Peel Region's 8,000 MPa / 150 MPa reinforced minimums, if bidding UV or reinforced hot water/steam work there (Lesson 27) — the generic 1,724 MPa comparison does not apply
