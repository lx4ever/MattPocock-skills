Trenchless Rehab Methods — Quick Reference

Print this. Glance at it before a client call.

Remember: there are only three real families below. "UV-cured lining" is a curing option inside CIPP, not a fourth peer.
Method What goes in Becomes the wall by Access points Real cost driver
CIPP
(hot water / steam / ambient)		 Resin-soaked felt or fiberglass tube Inverted or pulled in, calibrated, then cured (hours) One (inversion) or two (pull-in-place) Insertion method — bends push you toward pull-in-place
CIPP
(UV-cured)		 Resin-soaked fiberglass tube (light-sensitive resin) Same insertion, cured in minutes by a towed UV light train — no water Same as CIPP above Still insertion method; cure choice affects speed/water use, not the family
SIPP Liquid polymer or geopolymer mortar — no tube at all Sprayed/spin-cast directly onto the wall, bonds by adhesion One (spray head travels through) Surface prep quality — adhesion is the entire structure
Fold-and-form Real solid PE/PVC pipe, factory-folded (H/U/C shape) Winched in, then thermoformed round with steam + air, cooled to set — no resin, no cure chemistry Two (winched between manholes) Host pipe diameter accuracy — liner is sized 2–2.5% under on purpose

The one mix-up to avoid

Fold-and-form and CIPP pull-in-place both get winched in from two manholes on a cable — identical so far. The tell is what happens after: CIPP gets calibrated with pressure and chemically cured; fold-and-form gets thermoformed with steam and air, then just cooled. No resin, ever, in fold-and-form.

Shared closing steps

All three methods finish the same way: laterals that got covered during install are reopened with a robotic cutter (lateral reinstatement), and a final CCTV run confirms the line before sign-off.
