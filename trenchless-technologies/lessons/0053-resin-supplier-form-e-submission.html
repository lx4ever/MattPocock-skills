Lesson 53: Building a Resin Supplier's Submission — Form E and the Design Data Trail

A real municipal bid form that requires exactly the data this workspace has spent since Lesson 39 explaining a resin TDS can't supply on its own — and a direct, honest check of where Sino Polymer's own catalog stands against it.

Form E ("CIPP, CIPPSR Material Properties Form") is short — two pages — but it's the single most concrete document in this whole tender for a resin supplier. It's worth reading field by field, because its structure isn't arbitrary: it's built around exactly the neat-resin-vs-composite and short-term-vs-long-term gaps Lessons 39 and 41 already identified as the real difference between what's on a datasheet and what a design actually needs.

The CIPP table — three system slots, not one

Table 4E-1 requires a supplier to fill in data for up to three distinct lining systems in parallel columns:

SystemLabel as printed on the form
Lining System 1Styrene
Lining System 2Styrene Free
Lining System 3UV — Optional

For each system, the form asks for: Lining System Type (CIPP/GRP), Liner Tube (Bag) Manufacturer, Resin Type, Resin Manufacturer, Resin Manufacturer's Number, and then — the load-bearing part — Design Flexural Strength and Design Flexural Modulus, each reported separately as Short Term and Long Term.

This is a real bid form structurally requiring exactly what Lessons 39 and 41 flagged as missing from a typical TDS. Lesson 41's own six-step workflow named "get or estimate the long-term (creep-corrected) value" as the step a resin TDS alone never supplies. Form E doesn't ask for a single flexural modulus figure — it has a dedicated Long Term column standing right next to Short Term, on the actual submission document a real municipality requires. A supplier who only has an initial (short-term) neat-resin number, the kind that fills most of a marketing brochure, has a visibly incomplete form the moment the Long Term columns are compared against what's actually available.

Note also that UV is explicitly marked "Optional" — a supplier isn't required to offer a UV system at all, but if one is offered, it goes through the identical Short Term / Long Term data requirement as the other two.

The evidence bar: 5 reports, 3 projects, not lab data alone

For each system entered, the Supplier must include five lab testing reports, in English, for CIPP field installation samples — not neat-resin lab castings — produced by the Supplier on a minimum of three different previous projects using the same materials as proposed. CIPPSR gets an identical requirement in its own parallel table (4E-2), with two system slots instead of three and no explicit styrene-free/UV column labels — though the storm-sewer-to-surface-water styrene-free trigger from TS 466.05.02 still applies regardless of how the table itself is labeled.

"Field installation samples" is the specific, important word choice. This isn't a request for the resin's own neat-cast lab data — it's asking for evidence from the actual installed composite, on real prior jobs, with the same materials being proposed here. That's the same neat-resin-vs-composite distinction Lesson 41 built its entire "worked illustration of exactly where the TDS stops being enough" section around, now showing up as a literal bid requirement rather than an abstract design-methodology point.

What comes after the bid — the resin manufacturer's information package

Form E gets the tender-stage submission, but Part 3's own submittals list (due within 10 working days of the Commencement Date, before any lining can start) requires a second, deeper package specifically about the resin:

  • Resin manufacturer, type, and identification number
  • Resin-to-felt ratio and excess resin percentage, calculated in accordance with ASTM F1216-07a — the same specific older edition Peel Region's own spec names (Lesson 26), now confirmed as the edition this Toronto contract references too, not an isolated Peel choice.
  • A certified original copy from the resin manufacturer of the infrared spectrograph of the catalyzed resin mixture proposed — the exact FTIR reference-spectrum requirement this workspace has tracked since Lessons 22–23 (OPSS.MUNI 443's own certified-spectrum clause) and confirmed Sino Polymer has in-house capability for (Lesson 45's equipment list).
  • A certified original copy of cure temperature requirements, plus an example resin volume calculation and an example cure time calculation.
A concrete, worth-remembering fact: two independent municipalities, in two different specs, now cite F1216-07a by name for the same resin-to-felt ratio calculation. That's real corroboration that F1216-07a specifically — not just "F1216" generically — is a live, currently-cited edition in Ontario municipal practice, even though Lesson 49 confirmed it's an old, superseded edition of the actual standard. The design equations themselves haven't changed since (Lesson 49's verified worked example), so this isn't a technical gap — it's simply the edition municipal specs happen to keep citing.

Checking Sino Polymer's own catalog against this exact form

This is a genuinely useful, honest exercise: walk Form E's requirements against what this workspace already knows about Sino Polymer's real product line.

Form E requirementWhat Sino Polymer's catalog currently supports
Short-term flexural modulus/strength, Styrene or Styrene-Free systemYes — the original brochure's neat-resin data (Lesson 11), and MFE 701-01T/MERICAN 9900-01T specifically cover the styrene-free slot
Long-term flexural modulus/strengthOnly for MERICAN 9405T specifically, via the real third-party ISO 10468 creep test (Lesson 42) — not confirmed for the rest of the product line, and not literally ASTM D2990 as the F1216-07a submittal path would suggest
UV system, short and long term (optional slot)Short-term data exists for the UV line (9515, 9516, MFE 775UV/752UV); no long-term creep data confirmed for any UV-specific product in this workspace
5 field-installation lab reports, 3 projectsReal composite (not neat-resin) data now exists for a UV-CIPP hose product (Lesson 43) — genuinely relevant evidence, though tested per GB/T 1447-2005 rather than a North American standard, and not yet confirmed as five separate reports across three distinct projects
The honest read: Sino Polymer could fill in the Short Term columns confidently today, and has one real, credible long-term data point (9405T) to build the rest of the form around — but filling in every Long Term cell across a full three-system Form E submission would require exactly the additional testing this workspace has flagged as still open since Lesson 41. That's not a weakness unique to Sino Polymer — it's the same gap the entire CIPP resin industry has around long-term composite data generally. But it does mean Form E is a concrete, specific target to hand a supplier: which cells can be filled from what already exists, and which cells define exactly what new testing to commission next.
