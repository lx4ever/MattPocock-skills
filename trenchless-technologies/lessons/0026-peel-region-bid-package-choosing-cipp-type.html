Lesson 26: Dissecting a Real Bid Package

Regional Municipality of Peel's CIPP specs — four documents, and the first job is figuring out which one actually governs a given piece of pipe.

This is a real, complete bid document package: three parallel technical specifications for three different CIPP applications, plus a supplemental document that quietly rewrites parts of the other two. Reading a package like this starts with mapping the documents to the job, not reading page one to the end.

The four documents

DocumentCoversStatus
Appendix 5.9Mainline CIPP, hot water or steam cureFinal, November 2021
Appendix 5.10Mainline CIPP, UV light cureDraft, April 2021
Appendix 5.11CIPPSR — spot repair, not full-length liningDraft, May 2021
Appendix 5.16Supplemental amendments — overrides specific clauses in both 5.9 and 5.10Applies on top of the above
Read order matters. 5.16 isn't optional background reading — it explicitly rewrites named clauses in 5.9 and 5.10 (adds grouting to scope, replaces the public notification clause with a detailed protocol table, adds a storm-sewer resin restriction, and more). A bid built from 5.9 and 5.10 alone, without checking 5.16, would miss real requirements.

Choosing between the three CIPP types

  • Full-length mainline, hot water/steam cure (5.9): the default choice for a full section rehab where cure-time economics (Lesson 9) aren't the deciding factor.
  • Full-length mainline, UV cure (5.10): the fast-turnaround option from Lesson 9 — same underlying method, chosen when downtime matters more, and it always pairs with a fiberglass-reinforced liner (Lesson 6).
  • CIPPSR — spot repair (5.11): a completely different scope. Not a full pipe segment — a localized liner sized to a specific defect. Its own design rules, its own minimum thickness floor, and a much tighter service-disruption window (more in Lesson 28).

A direct payoff of Lesson 12's "watch the edition" point

All three specs lock design to one specific edition: ASTM F1216-07a, Appendix X1. Each document says outright: "Design by later versions of ASTM F1216 is not acceptable." Not the current edition (F1216-22, per Lesson 12) — a specific 2007 edition. This isn't an oversight; it's because Peel Region's own design parameters (enhancement factor, load assumptions) were validated against that edition's specific buckling formula. A design submitted using a newer edition's method would be non-compliant here, even though it's the more current standard.

When the design method changes entirely: non-circular pipe

The supplemental spec (5.16) adds a completely different design pathway for non-circular host pipe (egg-shaped, oval, deformed), using ASCE MOP 145 instead of ASTM F1216 X1 — and which sub-method applies depends directly on ovality:

Measured ovalityDesign approach
Less than 10%MOP 145 "State II" design
10% to 20%MOP 145 "State III" design
Greater than 20%Not suitable for MOP at all — full finite element analysis (FEA) required
This is where Lesson 9's field data becomes directly actionable. The IKT research cited there found real removed CIPP samples deforming up to 23% ovality — above this spec's 20% threshold for standard design methods entirely. A pipe genuinely that deformed doesn't just need a more conservative design input; it needs a fundamentally different, more expensive engineering exercise (custom FEA) to design for at all. "Check the actual ovality" isn't a minor caution — it can change which design process even applies.

Real numbers that diverge from the generic Ontario baseline

Compare Peel Region's actual design defaults against OPSS.MUNI 443's generic figures (Lesson 22) — worth doing every time, not assuming they match:

None of these differences are dramatic on their own, but they're real — and they're exactly why "meets Ontario standards" (Lesson 13's original point) always needs the follow-up question: whose standard, in this specific document, right now.

Quick check

Choosing the right document and method

1. A design submission uses the current ASTM F1216-22 edition for a Peel Region job. Compliant?

2. Field verification finds a host pipe at 22% ovality. What does that trigger?

3. A bidder builds their proposal from Appendix 5.9 and 5.10 only, treating Appendix 5.16 as optional background. What's the risk?
