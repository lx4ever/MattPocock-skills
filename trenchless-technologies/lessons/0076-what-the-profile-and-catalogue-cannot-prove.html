Lesson 76: What the Profile and Catalogue Can’t Prove, and What Only the Manufacturer Can Hand Over

The documents show facilities and quality-system claims a buyer can check — and leave every performance proof in Form E and Appendix 5.14 unaddressed.

Lessons 66 and 70 listed what the two tenders require of a resin maker. This lesson holds Sino Polymer’s profile and catalogue up against that list.

What the manufacturer can plausibly produce

Both tenders ask for things only the resin maker holds. The profile suggests Sino Polymer has the facilities for several of them:

The CIPP lines these documents add

  • Hot water or steam: MERICAN 9405T (“ISO-type”) and 9602, described in the profile as for the “flipping” (inversion) method.
  • UV: MERICAN 9515 (“ISO-NPG type”, catalogue) and 9515T and 9516, described as for the “drag-in” (pull-in) method.
  • Point repair: MERICAN 116A/B — the only point-repair system in the profile, which would sit under Peel’s spot-repair spec (Appendix 5.21, Item C-1) rather than the lined-segment items.
  • Styrene-free: MFE 701-01 (“for pultrusion, CIPP application, food grade”), the one the catalogue puts in the styrene-free row beside the chemical anchor resin.

Lesson 44 already listed most of these from the larger technical deck; the new details are the catalogue’s wording of what each is for and 116A/B.

Claims to treat carefully

  • Approvals are for other markets. UL 1316 / UL 1746 (double-wall fuel tanks), DNV, Lloyds and EN 45545-2 (rail fire) are real credentials but not municipal-sewer ones.
  • “Breaking the monopoly of Ashland, DSM” and market-share statements are the company’s own, unverified.
  • Customers shown (a GRP-pipe maker in Saudi Arabia, a polymer-concrete drainage maker in Germany, mining and steel groups) show bulk, long-running supply but no CIPP contractor in North America.
Update after the September 30 decks. The 0930 speaker notes agree with this lesson’s caution: they warn presenters that “a food-contact statement is not automatically a drinking-water approval”, not to imply NSF/ANSI/CAN 61 without an exact current listing, and that the profile does not substantiate every listed UL 94 grade. Lesson 78 works through those claims row by row.
The takeaway for the workspace. These documents close no open item from the tenders. What they add is a clearer split: facilities and quality-system claims are plausible and checkable (ask for the spectrograph and the ISO certificates), while Ontario history, long-term data and accredited field-sample reports must come from elsewhere.
