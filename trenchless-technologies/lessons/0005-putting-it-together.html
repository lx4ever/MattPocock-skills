Lesson 5: Putting It Together

All four terms, one client conversation.

You've now covered how CIPP, SIPP, and fold-and-form each become the new pipe wall, and where UV-cured lining actually sits. This lesson is the interleaving pass — mixing all of them together the way a real client conversation would, so the distinctions hold up under pressure, not just in isolation.

The 60-second version

A version you could actually say out loud:

"There are three ways we rehab a pipe without digging it up. CIPP pulls in a resin-soaked tube and hardens it in place — we can cure that with hot water, steam, or UV light, UV being the fast, no-water option. SIPP skips the tube entirely and sprays polymer straight onto the cleaned pipe wall, so surface prep is everything. And fold-and-form is a real plastic pipe, folded small, winched in, and steamed back round — no resin involved at all. Which one fits depends on your pipe's condition and shape."

Scenario practice

Each of these is the kind of half-sentence a client or field report actually gives you. Name the method.

Mixed recall

1. "No tube or pipe went in — they sprayed it straight onto the wall and it bonded there."

2. "A resin-soaked fiberglass tube went in, and they cured the whole thing in under twenty minutes with a light train."

3. "They winched a folded PVC pipe in from two manholes, steamed it back round, and cooled it — no resin at any point."

4. A client pushes back on extra cleaning charges for a badly corroded pipe before lining even starts. Which method are you almost certainly quoting, and why does prep matter that much?
