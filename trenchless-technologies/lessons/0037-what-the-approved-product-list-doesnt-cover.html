Lesson 37: What the Approved Product List Doesn't Cover

The earliest research into this whole tender — before any lesson in this workspace existed — flagged that Peel's approved-product lists "do not appear to identify approved CIPP resin brands." These two documents are the actual primary-source confirmation of that claim.

Appendices 5.30 (Wastewater) and 5.31 (Roads and Storm) are Peel's standing lists of pre-approved manufacturers and products, updated May 2025 — the reference a designer or contractor checks before specifying materials for new construction. Neither one mentions a single CIPP resin, liner tube, or lining system by name.

What's actually on these lists

Both documents are organized the same way: product category, governing specification (CSA/ASTM/OPSS/AWWA), a table of named manufacturers, and their specific model or product line. The categories are exclusively new pipe and hardware:

  • Main-line non-pressure pipe (PVC solid wall and profile/ribbed wall, HDPE, concrete, vitrified clay) and service connections
  • Pressure pipe/forcemains (PVC, HDPE, concrete pressure pipe, ductile iron fittings)
  • Saddles, couplings, joint restraints, repair clamps, service plugs, test fittings
  • Air release valves, check valves (including duckbill types), casing spacers
  • Precast maintenance holes, frames and covers, adjustment units, waterproofing, corrosion protection, venting hardware, steps and anchors
  • (Roads/Storm only) culvert pipe, catch basins, subdrain, noise walls, and tactile walking surface indicators for accessibility compliance
Nowhere in either list is there a category for CIPP resin, liner felt/tube, or a lining system. This confirms, directly from the primary source, what the original research into this opportunity could only infer: a resin manufacturer doesn't get "added to a Peel list" the way a PVC pipe manufacturer does. Lessons 26–27 already established the real mechanism — a resin gets evaluated as part of a specific contractor's complete liner system, submitted and reviewed project by project. These two documents are the concrete evidence that no alternate, simpler path (get pre-approved once, sell into any future Peel contract) exists for CIPP resin the way it does for a PVC pipe brand.

Two genuinely different quality-assurance models, side by side

Comparing how these lists qualify products against how Lessons 26–27 described CIPP resin qualification reveals a real structural difference in how the industry handles quality assurance for different product types:

New pipe (this list)CIPP liner system (Lessons 26–27)
What's qualifiedA factory-made product, by brand and modelA complete field-assembled composite (resin + tube + cure method), as a system
When it's qualifiedOnce, at the manufacturing plant — see the "Prequalification Stamp" belowPer project, using that project's actual resin/fibre ratio and design parameters
How long it stays validStanding — stays on the list until removed or updatedNot standing — Lesson 26 flagged a resin substitution can require new trial liners, independent testing, and Peel's written approval even for an established contractor
The "Prequalification Stamp" detail: concrete pipe on both lists must be "manufactured at a prequalified plant and marked with a Prequalification Stamp (triangular symbol)" — a real, physical, plant-level certification mark. It's a genuinely different model from CIPP's approach: a factory-made product gets certified once at the source and every unit off that line carries the mark, while a CIPP liner is a system built fresh on each job site, which is exactly why it can't be pre-certified the same way.

Two categories that connect back to earlier lessons, worth knowing exist

  • Corrosion and MIC protection products (Denso Paste/Tape, Petro Guard, Cem-Kote Flex CR, Cretex Easyseal): these protect maintenance hole concrete and pipe exteriors from microbially induced corrosion — the same H2S/sulfuric-acid environment that drives the resin corrosion-resistance discussion in Lesson 27, but applied to structure exteriors rather than the interior liner itself.
  • Hydrophilic grout for maintenance hole waterproofing (Multi-Gel): the same broad chemistry family as Lesson 30's chemical injection grouting, but used here to seal maintenance hole joints and precast sections against groundwater infiltration — a genuinely different application of a related technology, confirming hydrophilic/acrylate-type grouting shows up in more than one place in a real sewer rehabilitation program.
