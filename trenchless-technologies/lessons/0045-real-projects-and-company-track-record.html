Consistent with Lesson 11's own caution: these are genuine field installations with real locations, dates, and dimensions — a different, stronger category of evidence than lab test data alone. All three are in China (Shanghai area specifically), which matters for a straightforward reason: none of them constitute Ontario or North American installation history, the specific "Ontario experience" requirement Lesson 26 flagged as a real qualification hurdle. Real project history and regionally-relevant project history are both worth having, and they aren't the same thing.

The broader customer list — real, but mostly not CIPP

The document lists real, named industrial customers with specific certification and bulk-purchasing dates going back decades:

  • AMIANTIT Group (Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Europe, North Africa) — fiberglass anti-corrosion pipe, epoxy vinyl ester resin, certified 2010, bulk procurement since 2011
  • ACO Group (Germany, 30 production sites worldwide) — resin concrete gutter systems, certified 2009
  • Baosteel Group and Kanegawa Group — anti-corrosion structures/FRP products, certified 1995 and 1997 respectively — nearly three decades of continuous supply relationships
  • Formosa Plastics Group (Taiwan) — anti-corrosion structure/FRP products, certified 2005
  • A large additional logo list spanning PetroChina, Sinopec, Bayer, BASF, CNOOC, Shell, Volkswagen, SAIC-GM, several major Chinese state industrial groups, and academic/research partners including Donghua University
Read this honestly, not as CIPP credibility. Every one of these named relationships is for a different resin application — anti-corrosion coatings, FRP electrolytic cells, gutter systems, carbon fiber composites — not CIPP pipe lining specifically. What this list actually proves is that Sino Polymer/Huachang is a real, established industrial resin manufacturer with long-standing (in several cases 20–30 year) relationships with major, recognizable industrial names, going back to the 1990s. That's a genuine company-stability and manufacturing-credibility signal — useful context for "is this a real, viable long-term supplier" — but it should never be presented or mistaken for CIPP-specific track record, which is the separate, narrower claim the three project cases above actually support.

Two other credibility data points, correctly scoped

  • JEC Composites Innovation Award (2018) and a China Composites Exhibition award (2019) — both for "UV Curing Prepreg and Its Construction Technology," a real, named, internationally-recognized composites-industry award won two consecutive years. This is specifically about the UV-curing prepreg/hose technology, so it does connect fairly directly to the CIPP UV-curing product line, unlike the broader industrial customer list.
  • In-house R&D and testing capability — the source document lists a described in-house lab: FTIR, GPC, DMA, DSC, Karl Fischer titration, TGA, HDT testing, and a universal testing machine, plus a composite molding process center (pultrusion, vacuum introduction, 3D printing, light-curing equipment). This is a real, concrete answer to "can this company generate more of the missing test data on its own, or does it depend entirely on outsourced one-off tests" — the equipment list suggests genuine in-house capability, including FTIR specifically, the exact instrument needed for the certified reference spectrum requirement flagged since Lessons 22–23.

How to use this in a real conversation

The honest, defensible version of this evidence, layered correctly: "This is an established manufacturer with a real, multi-decade industrial track record and in-house testing capability (company credibility) that has also completed real, large-scale CIPP installations, including 20+ km of sewer main rehabilitation in one program (CIPP-specific track record) — though not yet in Ontario or North America specifically (the honest gap, per Lesson 26's Ontario-experience finding)." Collapsing all three layers into one undifferentiated "trusted supplier" claim would overstate what any single piece of evidence actually shows — exactly the kind of imprecision this workspace has flagged and corrected before (the OPSS.MUNI citation error, the neat-resin-vs-composite conflation).
