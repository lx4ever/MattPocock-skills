Lesson 79: Reading a Real Lab Report: The SGS Extractables Test on MFE 711

A real lab report passes a food-contact extractables test for MFE 711 — and it is worth knowing exactly how little that proves for a sewer or watermain liner.

The folder’s sixth PDF looks like another safety data sheet but is an independent test report. Reading one line by line is the best way to learn what a “Pass” covers.

First, what was and wasn’t in the SDS folder

The folder holds five safety data sheets — MERICAN 3222 A and B, MERICAN 9406, MFE 711 and MFE 7516 — all read in full in Lesson 58. A sixth PDF, SHRT26000374R01_EN.pdf, looks like it belongs with them but is not an SDS. It is a three-page test report from SGS-CSTC Standards Technical Services (Shanghai), and it had not been read directly before this lesson.

Both tests are marked Pass, under a stated decision rule (simple acceptance, ILAC-G8:09/2019). “ND” means the amount was below the lab’s reporting limit, not that it was zero. The limit and reporting limit printed are both 0.1 mg/in², so the result says nothing was detected at or above that level.

What this does and doesn’t show

  • It shows that one cured specimen of MFE 711, tested by an independent lab (SGS) in April 2026, released no detectable extractives into water or heptane under those two conditions.
  • It does not cover other simulants, other grades, the cure schedule or thickness of the tablet, or long-term use. The report does not say how the tablet was made.
  • It is not an FDA approval. It is a laboratory result against one regulation’s test method. (To my knowledge 21 CFR 177.2420 concerns cross-linked polyester resins in food-contact articles; I did not re-read the rule.)
  • It is not drinking-water evidence. NSF/ANSI/CAN 61 is a different scheme with different tests and listings (Lessons 44 and 78).
  • It is not CIPP evidence. MFE 711 is the bisphenol-A vinyl ester sold as a best seller for FRP pipes and tanks (Lesson 75), not one of the CIPP resins.
Two corrections this triggers. (1) Lesson 44 said an SGS report had “confirmed” FDA food-contact status for a styrene-free UV-curing product line; that wording came from the larger technical deck. The primary report now in hand covers MFE 711 and 21 CFR 177.2420 only, so Lesson 44 carries a note saying so. (2) The 0930 deck’s “FDA approved” entry sits on the drinking-water row for MERICAN 9515N-01, 3505 and MFE 701-01T. Its own presenter check says to cite per grade. This report is exactly that kind of citation — but for a different grade, which is not on that row.
