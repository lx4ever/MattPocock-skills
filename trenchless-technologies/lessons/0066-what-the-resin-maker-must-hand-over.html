Lesson 66: What the Resin Maker Must Hand Over: Form E, the Resin Submittal, and the Styrene Rule

Form E is familiar. The certified resin submittal and the empty styrene-free column are what is new.

Everything a resin supplier needs to prepare for this tender fits on a short list — and the list is clearer than it was in 2025 about what only the manufacturer can provide.

Deliverable 1 — Form E, the material properties form

Form E (“CIPP Material Properties Form”) is mandatory; omit it and the bid is non-compliant. Table 4E-1 has three lining-system columns:

SlotLabel printed on the form
Lining System 1(Styrene)
Lining System 2(Styrene Free)
Lining System 3(Reinforced)

For each system the bidder names the tube manufacturer and resin (type, manufacturer, manufacturer’s number), says whether it is CIPP or UV CIPP, and gives design flexural strength and modulus, short term and long term. The evidence bar is the same as Lesson 53’s: five lab reports, in English, on field-installed samples from at least three previous projects using the same materials, tested by an accredited laboratory (the form names Paragon Systems as an example).

What changed since 2025: the third column is now “Reinforced” instead of “UV — Optional”, with the system-type row reading “CIPP or UVCIPP”, and the form no longer mentions CIPPSR spot repairs because this contract has none. Form E says minimum flexural modulus and strength follow “the technical specifications in Part 3”. I did not find numeric minimums in the Part 3 text supplied — they presumably live in the City’s base TS 4.10 specification, which this packet amends but does not reproduce. That is worth confirming before comparing any resin against a threshold.

Deliverable 2 — the manufacturer-certified resin submittal

Part 3 Section 1 (“CIPP Resin Manufacturer’s Information”) requires the Contractor to provide:

  • resin manufacturer, type and identification number;
  • resin-to-felt ratio and excess resin percentage per ASTM F1216;
  • a certified original copy from the resin manufacturer of the infrared spectrograph of the catalyzed resin mixture proposed for the contract;
  • a certified original copy from the manufacturer of the cure temperature requirements, an example resin volume calculation and an example cure time calculation.

These are things only the resin maker can produce. The example cure-time calculation partly addresses the cure-time disclosure gap flagged in Lessons 15 and 46 — though an example calculation for one liner is not the same as a published gel time.

The styrene-free rule, with an important difference from 2025

Lesson 54 found 14 of 305 segments flagged “Styrene Free Resin: Y” in the 2025 repair list. In this tender the column exists, but it is blank on every row of both sheets. So no segment is pre-designated styrene-free. What remains is the proximity rule (TS 4.10.22 item 7): if a sewer’s upstream or downstream manhole is within 100 m of a hospital, school or day care during operating hours, senior home or medical facility, the Contractor must not proceed with styrene resin, must notify the Contract Administrator, and proceeds with non-styrene resin (or epoxy) once approved. Separately, the Contractor must write an overarching styrene management procedure, citing “performing UV CIPP lining whereas feasible” and “high-performance pre-liners” as examples.

What follows (partly my inference). Styrene-free here is a contingency, not planned scope — a contractor should be ready to switch on any segment, which is presumably why Form E asks for a styrene-free system with its own test data. The spec says sewers identified as styrene-free are paid under separate price items, but none are identified, and the documents I read don’t say how a mid-job switch under the 100 m rule is paid. That is a question for the City before pricing.
