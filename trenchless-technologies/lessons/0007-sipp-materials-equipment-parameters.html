Lesson 7: SIPP — Materials, Equipment, Parameters

Four material families, one piece of equipment that makes or breaks the job, and the parameter that tells you how fast the line comes back online.

SIPP's materials decision is really a trade-off between how fast the coating sets and how much structural work it can do. That trade-off drives the equipment and the numbers worth knowing.

Materials

  • Epoxy — common for potable water and lighter corrosion work. Slower set than polyurea, generally lower cost.
  • Polyurethane — a middle ground between epoxy and polyurea on set speed and flexibility.
  • Polyurea — sets extremely fast, often seconds to minutes, and stays elastomeric (flexible). The go-to when a line can't stay offline long.
  • Geopolymer mortar — a fiber-reinforced cementitious material, not a polymer coating at all. Used for large-diameter storm and sanitary structures that need real structural rebuilding, not just a corrosion barrier.
Sales-relevant point: "SIPP" isn't one material — it's a spray-application method that four very different material families share. Polyurea's speed is why it gets chosen for high-traffic lines; geopolymer's structural mass is why it gets chosen for a badly deteriorated storm culvert. Neither is a "better" SIPP — they solve different problems, and a client's real question ("how fast" vs. "how structurally compromised") points to the material, not the other way around.

Equipment

The core piece of equipment is plural-component spray equipment — a proportioning pump unit (examples in the field: Graco XM, Gusmer H-2000/H-3500, Glas-Craft MH) that meters and mixes two or more reactive components at a fixed ratio, then delivers them through an impingement-mix spray gun. That gun rides a robotic carriage, connected by an umbilical hose, that travels the pipe applying the coating — the same rig from Lesson 3, described here at the equipment level.

Parameters worth knowing

ParameterTypical figure
Mix ratioFixed by the manufacturer's formulation — commonly cited around 1:1 by volume for polyurea, though this varies by product
Total coating thicknessCommonly up to ~3–3.5 mm for gravity sewer work; force main coatings can spec up to ~500 mils depending on the system
Set / cure timePolyurea: seconds to minutes. Epoxy and polyurethane: notably longer — the real driver of how fast a line returns to service
Number of passesApplied in one or more controlled passes to build to spec thickness, monitored continuously as it goes
