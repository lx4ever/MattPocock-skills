Lesson 3: How SIPP Is Sprayed On

No tube, no insertion-method choice — SIPP's real decision point is upstream of the spray gun.

Recall SIPP's tell from Lesson 1: no tube goes in, ever. A robotic head sprays or spin-casts polymer or geopolymer mortar straight onto the cleaned pipe wall, and it bonds there by adhesion alone. That "by adhesion alone" detail changes which step in the process actually matters most — and it isn't the one you'd expect from CIPP.

The six steps

  1. Inspect. A CCTV run assesses corrosion, wear, and structural condition, and scopes exactly what the spray job needs to cover.
  2. Clean & prepare the surface. Hydro-jetting and mechanical descaling strip rust, scale, and debris down to a clean, dry, bondable surface — then a second CCTV run confirms it's actually ready. This step carries more weight in SIPP than anywhere in CIPP.
  3. Bypass the flow. Temporary pumping reroutes wastewater around the work zone so the crew has a dry, controlled pipe to spray into.
  4. Spray application. The robotic spray head travels through the pipe, applying the coating in one or more controlled passes. Mix ratio, temperature, flow rate, liner thickness, and travel speed are monitored continuously to hold a uniform coat — commonly up to about 3–3.5 mm.
  5. Cure. The polymer sets through its own chemistry (or, for geopolymer mortar, its own hydration reaction) rather than an external heat source doing the work. Curing is monitored, not driven, the way CIPP's hot-water or UV cure is.
  6. Reinstate laterals & final inspection. Any branch connection the spray covered gets reopened with a robotic cutter, and a final CCTV run confirms a seamless, pinhole-free coat end to end.
Sales-relevant point: in CIPP, the decision that changes the quote is how the tube gets in (inversion vs. pull-in-place). In SIPP, there's no tube to insert — so that decision doesn't exist. The decision that changes a SIPP quote is how much surface prep the pipe needs. A heavily corroded, scaled-up pipe needs more cleaning passes before it'll take a proper bond, and that's real, billable work — not padding. A CIPP tube has some tolerance for an imperfect host wall because it's a structural tube in its own right; a SIPP coating has none, because adhesion is the entire structure.
