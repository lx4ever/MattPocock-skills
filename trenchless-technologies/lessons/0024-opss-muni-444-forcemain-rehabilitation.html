Lesson 24: OPSS.MUNI 444 — Forcemain Rehabilitation

"Construction Specification for Sewage Forcemain Rehabilitation by CIPP Liner" — 443, plus everything pressure adds.

444 shares its structure and most of its text directly with 443 (Lesson 22) — the definitions even say "as defined in OPSS 443." The interesting part is entirely in the delta: a forcemain is sewage under pressure, not gravity flow, and every difference between these two documents traces back to that one fact.

Scope: sewage forcemains specifically — pressure pipe, not the gravity sanitary/storm sewers 443 covers. Same CIPP method, genuinely different engineering problem.

What pressure adds to the design (444.04.01)

Every design parameter from 443 carries over unchanged — 50-year life, safety factor 2, groundwater at surface, 6.9 MPa soil modulus, the same live-load trucks, 2% default ovality, 3 m minimum depth. Pressure adds two more:

  • Design operating pressure, as specified in the Contract Documents.
  • Vacuum of minus 101 kPa — full vacuum, treated as an external pressure in the design per F1216, alongside groundwater, soil, and live load.

Engineering design now has to follow "current ASTM and AWWA design standards for pressure pipe rehabilitation" — AWWA enters the picture specifically because this is pressure pipe, matching the AWWA reference already seen for SIPP potable-water work in Lesson 7.

A third materials minimum: tensile strength

443 required two minimums: flexural modulus (1,724 MPa) and flexural strength (31 MPa). 444 adds a third:

Minimum tensile strength: 21 MPa, tested per ASTM D638 or ASTM D2290 (Apparent Hoop Tensile Strength of Plastic or Reinforced Plastic Pipe). This makes sense once you see the loading difference: 443's gravity pipe is a bending problem (flexural properties dominate); 444's pressurized forcemain also has to resist hoop stress trying to burst it outward — a tensile problem flexural numbers don't capture.

New submission requirements

  • A temporary forcemain bypass plan — not needed for 443's gravity sewers, but a pressurized forcemain generally can't just run at reduced capacity during work the way a gravity line sometimes can.
  • For water- or steam-cured systems: a detailed discharge plan for releasing the curing medium to a receiver, including sampling, testing, and treatment where specified.

Stricter surface preparation

444.07.09 requires complete removal of any previous coating or lining unless it's tightly bonded and the manufacturer and Engineer both agree it can be safely lined over without compromising performance — a more conservative default than 443's general cleaning language.

A construction step 443 doesn't have: pressure testing

444.07.17, Hydrostatic Pressure and Leakage Testing: the lined forcemain has to be pressure tested for leakage at 120% of maximum operating pressure, with test duration and allowed makeup water per ASTM F1216 Section 8.3. Results are reported immediately to the Contract Administrator. There's no equivalent step in 443 at all — a gravity sewer doesn't get pressure-tested, because it was never designed to hold pressure in the first place.

Termination is stricter too: 444.07.16 requires the CIPP to be fully bonded and fully conforming to the host pipe, forming a leak-tight seal — not just "neat and free of obstructions" as in 443. A pressurized line can't tolerate the loose termination tolerance a gravity line can.

QA: 100% sampling, not 30%

444.08.03.01: samples are taken and tested from every CIPP installation — not the 30% sampling rate specified in 443. The consequence of an undetected defect in a pressurized sewage line — a burst, an uncontrolled discharge — is different in kind from a gravity sewer's failure mode, and the QA regime scales with it.
