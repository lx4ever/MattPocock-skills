Lesson 73: Time, Liquidated Damages, and the Warranty Holdback

$1,500 a day twice over, a holdback that can be released early only if the contractor pays for inspections, and a warranty the resin has to support.

The commercial terms of this tender mostly look like Peel’s earlier ones (Lesson 31), with a few additions the addenda introduced.

Time and the liquidated damages

TermPeel 2026-323T
Contract time294 Working Days from notice to commence

Start date riskThe anticipated start can change at the Region’s discretion, with no extra payment for seasonal conditions, winter work or bypass modifications caused by a late notice to proceed or award

Because the two damages are assessed independently, a contractor late on both could in principle owe both daily amounts — my reading, not stated in so many words. Toronto’s equivalent was $3,500 per day (Lesson 62).

Timing pressure is real: a bypass plan needs a 4-week review (Lesson 72) and the Phase 1 window runs through winter. Toronto’s 150 Working Days (Lesson 62) can be compared with 294 here for a longer, creek-side job.

The warranty and the 80% early release

  • Two-year warranty from substantial completion. Items B-9 and B-10 pay for V4 warranty CCTV inspections of sewers and manholes at the two-year review.
  • Warranty Holdback (Supplemental General Conditions, Appendix 5.28): a sliding formula on contract value — 10% of the first $50,000, then 5%, 4%, 3%, and 2% above $500,000 (with fixed maximums for the lower bands), held until the end of the warranty period.
  • Early release option: after 12 months the contractor may apply for release of 80% of the Warranty Holdback, if no deficiencies are outstanding and at least $5,000 remains held. Addendum 2 (section 23.1) adds that the contractor must first complete CCTV and manhole inspections or scans of all the work at its own cost, submit the reports, and repair any defects found at no cost. Those inspections do not replace the two-year ones.

Illustration with an invented contract value: for a $5.0M contract the formula gives $21,500 + 2% of the $4.5M above $500,000 = $111,500. Releasing 80% frees $89,200 and leaves $22,300 held. The cost of the extra CCTV and scanning has to be weighed against getting that cash back a year early.

Warranty exclusions and a spec inconsistency

Appendix 5.14 section 42.1 lets a contractor who judges that a liner can’t be warranted request a Warranty Exclusion Letter, which the Contract Administrator must approve before installation. Late requests are refused. The same appendix has section 42 (two-year warranty, CCTV one month before it ends) and section 43 (a one-year warranty with CCTV three months before the end). The special provision’s two-year terms and the order-of-precedence clause settle it, but it is another document mismatch like Lesson 67’s.

For the resin thread. Two years of liability plus an early-release option that depends on clean CCTV makes cure quality, not unit price, the contractor’s cash lever. A resin with documented long-term data (Lesson 70) and cure-monitoring evidence (Lesson 48) helps a contractor make the case that it can promise a warranty with fewer exclusions.
