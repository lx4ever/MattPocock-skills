Lesson 63: Who May Bid: Canadian Suppliers Only, and Where a Resin Maker Sits

The bidder has to be Canadian. The resin maker does not — but its resin still ends up on a form.

The first new rule in this tender sits near the top of Part 1 and decides who is even allowed to submit. It matters for the resin thread mostly because of who it does not restrict.

The eligibility rule

Part 1, Section 1.1.2: in accordance with the Canadian Free Trade Agreement (CFTA), bids are accepted only from Canadian Suppliers. Bids from Trade Partner Suppliers, Non-American Suppliers and US-Based Suppliers are deemed non-compliant and rejected.

A Canadian Supplier (Part 1 §3.14 and the Canadian-supplier attestation form) is a supplier, manufacturer or distributor that operates permanently in Canada and meets any one of three tests:

  1. Its headquarters or principal place of business is in a Canadian province or territory; or
  2. At least 70% of its employees are in Canada at the time of bid submission; or
  3. It is a Canadian Business Subsidiary — a corporation operating in Canada but controlled by a foreign parent, with permanent offices or production facilities here and at least 70% of the deliverables provided by Canada-based employees.

The folder holds three attestation forms (Canadian; Non-American and Trade Partner; Non-USA Based). My reading is that the City uses one family of forms across tenders and that, in this tender, only the Canadian path is eligible per §1.1.2 — the documents don’t say that about the other two forms in so many words. The successful supplier must hand over a signed attestation within two business days of the City asking, or the award can be rescinded (§2.1.4).

Who is “the supplier”?

Here it is the contractor, not the resin maker. Form C sets the bar for the bidder:

Form C requirementDetail
Volume6 km combined of gravity-sewer CIPP, across at least 3 projects finished in the last 5 years as general contractor
SizeAt least 2 of the 3 projects worth CAD $2,000,000 or more each (excluding taxes)
ShapeAt least 1 of the 3 includes egg-shaped or other non-circular CIPP (location, length and dimensions to be shown)
SafetyA valid OHSMS certification in the bidder’s legal name (for example IHSA COR 2020)

Part 1 §1.9.5.4 then caps any single subcontractor at 50% of the overall bid value. So a resin or felt supplier cannot be the prime bidder or a large subcontractor here; it supplies materials to a Canadian general contractor who carries the bid.

What this means for the resin-marketing thread. The sales target is exactly the kind of contractor Lessons 25 and 61 profiled — one that can already clear Form C. A contractor’s resin choice then shows up in two places where a bid can be won or lost: Form E (can the resin’s data meet the spec?) and the Domestic Supply Chain Plan (Lesson 64: where was the resin made?). A foreign resin maker is not shut out by the eligibility rule — the bidder must be Canadian — but the resin’s origin is visible to the City.
