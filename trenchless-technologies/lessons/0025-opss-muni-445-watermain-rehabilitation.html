Lesson 25: OPSS.MUNI 445 — Watermain Rehabilitation

"Construction Specification for Watermain Rehabilitation by CIPP Lining" — 444's pressure-pipe foundation, plus everything drinking water adds.

445 shares 444's pressure-design DNA (same F1216 X1 fully-deteriorated method, same 21 MPa tensile minimum, same pressure-testing logic) but adds a whole layer specific to potable water — and drops the sampling frequency lower than either of the other two, which is worth understanding rather than just memorizing.

Scope: watermains up to and including 400 mm diameter, with NSF 61 certification required — the specification says so directly in its scope line, not buried in a materials clause.

The same pressure-design core as 444

50-year life, safety factor 2, groundwater at surface, 6.9 MPa soil modulus, the same live-load trucks, 2% default ovality, minus 101 kPa vacuum. Two differences worth noting:

  • Minimum depth of 2 m, not 444's 3 m — watermains typically run shallower than forcemains.
  • A default design operating pressure of 862 kPa is actually given in the document — 444 only says "as specified in the Contract Documents." 445 supplies a concrete starting figure.

What drinking water adds on top

Blanket NSF/ANSI 61 requirement: "All materials used in the CIPP process that will make contact with potable water shall be NSF/ANSI Standard 61 certified" (445.05.01). Not "some products" the way the Sino Polymer brochure hedged in Lesson 11 — every contact material, required, full stop.

The finished liner also has to resist the water treatment chemicals actually present in the supply (chlorine, chloramine, etc.) — a requirement with no equivalent in 443 or 444, since sewage doesn't get chemically treated the same way drinking water does.

New submissions unique to 445

  • A disinfection and water sampling plan showing compliance with provincial regulations.
  • A temporary water supply plan, when specified.
  • NSF/ANSI 61 certification submitted directly for the specific watermain liner being used.
  • Manufacturer's data must show the product's capability — and limitations — lining through deflected joints in the existing watermain, and surviving ongoing future joint deflection once cured in place. This is a genuinely distinctive, forward-looking requirement: not just "does it install here," but "will it still hold up as this pipe keeps moving over the next 50 years."
  • A contingency plan specifically for contamination incidents — alongside the same damage/placement/product contingencies as 443 and 444.

A construction step neither 443 nor 444 has: disinfection

445.07.20 requires post-lining watermain disinfection per Ontario MECP procedures or the Contract Documents — standard practice before any drinking water line returns to service, and simply not applicable to a sewage line. Service connection reinstatement also gets extra care here (445.07.19): connections are cut neatly without damage that could affect performance over the design life, and plugged connections are specifically addressed rather than assumed away.

One administrative flexibility 443 and 444 don't have: the Contract Administrator may waive the requirement to submit the pre-lining video inspection before lining proceeds (445.07.10.01) — though the inspection still has to happen, and the Contractor's cleaning responsibility doesn't go away just because submission is deferred. If a post-lining deficiency shows up later, the pre-lining video can still be used to trace it back to inadequate prep.

QA: 20% — lower than either 443 or 444

Worth noting as a genuine pattern, not an explained rule: the document doesn't state why watermain sampling sits lower than gravity sewer sampling. A reasonable read is that NSF 61 certification and mandatory disinfection already provide a materials/safety control layer gravity sewer work doesn't have — but that's an inference from the pattern, not something the spec says outright. Worth confirming with an Ontario-experienced engineer before repeating it as documented fact.

Quick check

What drinking water changes

1. A resin brochure says "some products are NSF 61 certified" without specifying which. Is that sufficient for an OPSS.MUNI 445 job?

2. Why does 445 require manufacturer data on surviving future joint deflection, when 443 and 444 don't ask for this?

3. A crew finishes lining a watermain segment and moves straight to service connection reinstatement, skipping disinfection. Compliant?
