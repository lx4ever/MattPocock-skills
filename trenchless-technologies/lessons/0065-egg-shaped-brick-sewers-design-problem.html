Lesson 65: Egg-Shaped Brick Sewers: A Different Design Problem

25 of 37 segments are egg-shaped brick, and the design rules, the experience bar and the extra-thickness pay items all change because of it.

A circular pipe has one dimension. An egg-shaped brick sewer has several, and no two are identical — so the City’s documents treat the bid-stage design as an estimate and pay for the difference later.

Why the egg shape changes the work

Lessons 38–40 walked through ASTM F1216’s design for circular pipe. 25 of this tender’s 37 lined segments are not circular: 900×600 mm (22 segments) and 1050×675 mm (3), all in brick. The tender’s own design instructions (TS 4.10.25 amendments, Part 3 §3.2) switch the method:

RequirementWhat the spec says
Design methodPreliminary liner design at bid stage “in accordance with MOP145” (Lesson 26 explained the MOP 145 family for non-circular pipe)
Starting geometryAppendix 3.2-1, a LiDAR-scan table for the 25 egg sewers — “for preliminary design only”
Final designBased on geometry verified by laser profile or other means after the post-cleaning CCTV (V2); prepared, sealed and signed by the Contractor’s Ontario-licensed P.Eng.
Serviceability limitMaximum allowable deflection of the non-circular liner: 3%
HydraulicsA flow analysis per design using Manning’s formula with n = 0.012 for the CIPP and 0.016 for the existing sewer; lined capacity must be at least the existing

How irregular is “900×600”?

Appendix 3.2-1 reports four measurements per pipe: height H, width W, and two radii Rs and Rb (the appendix doesn’t define them; they are presumably the side and bottom radii of the egg profile). The 22 pipes sold as 900×600 actually scan like this:

MeasureRange across the 22 “900×600” pipes
Height H881–908 mm
Width W603–633 mm
Rs870–1124 mm
Rb165–226 mm

H and W stay within about 35 mm of nominal, but Rs varies by over 250 mm between pipes that share a nominal size. That is why the table carries a note that the survey data “shall not be relied upon for final design.” Brick is hand-built, and the liner thickness the design calls for depends on the actual shape.

How the contract prices that uncertainty

  • Items F.01.01–F.01.05, Increased CIPP Liner Thickness: if field conditions call for a thicker liner than the initial design, the extra is paid per metre for every 1.5 mm of added thickness. The Contractor must tell the Contract Administrator within 48 hours and wait for instructions; no liner may be installed that fails the actual field conditions.
  • Design approval gate: no lining starts until all standard designs are approved, and the Contract Administrator takes at least 3 working days per design review.
  • Form C: at least one of the bidder’s three reference projects must include egg-shaped or other non-circular CIPP.
Resin implication. For egg-shaped brick, the Form E long-term flexural numbers (Lesson 66) feed a non-circular design with a 3% deflection cap, not the circular F1216 equation. The Peel ovality tiers in Lesson 26 (under 10%, 10–20%, over 20%) come from Peel’s spec; this Toronto document doesn’t restate them, so don’t carry them over. And Lesson 56’s finding still applies: whether a segment needs a reinforced liner or standard felt comes from the structural design, which here depends on field geometry the bidder can’t fully know at bid time.
