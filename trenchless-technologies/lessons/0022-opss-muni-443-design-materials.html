Lesson 22: OPSS.MUNI 443 — Design & Materials

The real document, finally — and it's OPSS.MUNI 443, not the 460 this workspace had been citing.

"Construction Specification for Rehabilitation of Non-Pressure Pipe by Cured-In-Place Pipe (CIPP) Liner," OPSS.MUNI 443, November 2023. Every figure below comes directly from the primary document — the first time this workspace has had it in hand rather than working from search summaries.

Scope (443.01): covers sanitary and storm sewers, and explicitly all three CIPP application types together — full-length lining, spot repairs, and lateral lining. Not just mainline work.

References worth noting

Mostly the ASTM standards already established (F1216, F1743, D5813, D790), plus two worth flagging specifically:

  • ASTM F2019 — confirmed here as specifically the UV-cured glass-reinforced plastic CIPP standard, distinct from F1216/F1743.
  • ASTM D2990 — Standard Test Methods for Tensile, Compressive, and Flexural Creep and Creep-Rupture of Plastics. This is the long-term testing standard behind the creep-retention-factor concept from Lesson 19 — and OPSS.MUNI 443 requires third-party D2990 testing as part of the manufacturer's submitted technical data (443.04.02), not just a datasheet claim.
  • CSA S6:19 (Canadian Highway Bridge Design Code) — supplies the live-load design truck used in the structural design parameters below.

A new resin family: silica

443.03's definition of "resin" lists polyester, vinyl ester, epoxy, and silica as the thermoset families covered. Silica (silicate) resin hasn't come up anywhere else in this workspace — worth treating as a fourth family to research if it becomes relevant to a real job.

Design requirements (443.04.01) — real numbers

For circular host pipe, design follows ASTM F1216 — and OPSS.MUNI 443 makes an explicit choice Lesson 19 only described as an option:

Fully deteriorated condition is mandatory — not a design choice, a requirement. Ontario doesn't let a designer assume the host pipe shares any of the structural load.

Design life50 years
Safety factor2

Worth cross-checking against Lesson 9: the default ovality assumption here is 2% — but the IKT field research cited in Lesson 9 found real removed CIPP samples deforming up to 23% against a 3% design figure used elsewhere in the industry. OPSS.MUNI 443's 2% default is explicitly a fallback ("unless otherwise known to be greater") — meaning actual field-verified ovality data should override it, and given Lesson 9's findings, it often should be checked rather than assumed.

Non-circular host pipe (egg, oval, other non-round shapes) can't use the standard F1216 X1 method at all — a different design method has to be specified in the Contract Documents or approved by the Contract Administrator.

Submission requirements (443.04.02)

  1. Design package, 14 days before installation. Must show technical assumptions, the design method and formulas used, short-term test values and long-term design properties, and the resulting liner wall thickness. Installation conditions (pipeline dimensions, depth, water table, host pipe ovality) must be documented in full. The design work must bear the seal and signature of a licensed Engineer — not optional.
  2. Resident notification letter, also 14 days out. Must include the contract number, an emergency contact, the nature and expected duration of service interruption, start date, working hours, and the procedure residents should follow during install and cure.
  3. Work plan package, 7 days before starting work. A wetout plan, a CIPP cure plan, a liner sampling plan, a QA/QC plan, a lateral reinstatement plan, and the resident procedure — plus a personnel list (with backup personnel and their qualifications) and a traffic control plan.
  4. Manufacturer's technical data — the applicable ASTM standard, material composition and physical properties, transport/handling/storage recommendations, installation procedures, curing parameters (temperature, duration, cool-down), UV lamp specifics where relevant, and third-party ASTM D2990 creep testing — plus repair procedures for product damaged during installation.
  5. Contingency plans for five specific failure scenarios: damage to existing service connections, improper CIPP placement, damage to the host pipe, the CIPP failing to achieve structural integrity, and product damaged during installation.

Materials (443.05) — the numbers behind "meets F1216"

RequirementValue
Minimum flexural modulus1,724 MPa (tested per ASTM D790 or ISO 11296-4)
Minimum flexural strength31 MPa (same test methods)
These match the US figures exactly. 1,724 MPa ≈ 250,000 psi, and 31 MPa ≈ 4,500 psi — the same ASTM F1216 minimums cited from US sources back in Lesson 9. Ontario didn't set its own bar; it adopted the same one, just expressed in metric.

Where the actual design calls for values higher than these minimums — which is common — installed liner samples have to meet or exceed the design values, not just the code floor (more on this in Lesson 23's QA section). Preliners need Engineer approval and must have no detrimental effect on liner performance. The tube itself has to be marked with the manufacturer's name or ID symbol at intervals no more than 1.5 m apart — a direct traceability requirement.
