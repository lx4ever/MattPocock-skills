Lesson 11: Three Resin Pitches

Sino Polymer's vinyl ester line, pitched to three different buyers — worked examples from Lesson 10's framework applied to a real spec sheet.

Same source material, three different audiences. Each pitch below leads with a different one of Lesson 10's six value-prop levers, because each buyer is solving a different problem — leading with the wrong lever wastes the strongest thing you actually have to say.

1. MFE 701-01T — the drinking-water utility engineer

Buyer: A municipal water utility or their contractor, doing potable-water CIPP in an occupied area — near homes, schools, or anywhere a hot-water cure's odor becomes a resident-complaint problem.

Lead lever: Environmental / regulatory profile.

"For drinking water lines, MFE 701-01T is styrene-free with no irritating odor and certified non-volatile content — so you're not managing a work-zone air-quality problem or fielding resident complaints during cure. It's also formulated for fast curing, so your crews get more linear feet done per shift without the usual trade-off between speed and emissions."

Proof points: styrene-free, no irritating odor, certified non-volatile content, HDT 85°C, purpose-segmented for drinking water in Sino Polymer's own catalog.

Objection to have an answer ready for: "Is it NSF 61/51 certified?" The brochure only says some products carry that certification, without naming which. Confirm the certification scope for this specific SKU with Sino Polymer before answering yes — NSF 61/51 is a specific potable-water standard, and it's not the same claim as "FDA-referenced."

2. MFE 770T — the industrial/solvent-line contractor

Buyer: A contractor or facility engineer dealing with a high-temperature, chemically aggressive process line — refineries, chemical plants, heavy industrial sites — especially one where a previous liner failed early.

Lead lever: Structural performance headroom, specifically thermal.

"Most CIPP resins are specced for municipal sewage — ambient temperature, mild chemistry. MFE 770T is built for the line that already ate your last liner: a glass transition temperature above 150°C, a heat deflection temperature of 145–150°C, and specific engineered resistance to acidic, oxidizing environments and solvents. If your last liner softened or degraded early because the line runs hot or aggressive, this isn't a general-purpose resin stretched past its rating — it's built for that exact failure mode."

Proof points: Tg >150°C and HDT 145–150°C — the highest in Sino Polymer's entire CIPP lineup — plus documented resistance to acidic oxidizing environments and solvents, and good retention of strength/toughness at elevated temperature.

Objection to have an answer ready for: "These are resin datasheet numbers — what does the actual cured liner test at?" Fair pushback. ASTM D638/D790/D648 figures here are neat-resin properties, not the finished fiberglass/felt composite liner. For a structural commitment on a real job, get composite-liner test data at the actual fiber/resin ratio being used — not just the resin spec sheet.

3. The UV line (9515N-01 / 9516N-01) — the throughput-focused contractor

Buyer: A CIPP contractor running UV-cure crews, trying to fit more jobs into the week, or comparing UV economics against a hot-water cure line they're currently running.

Lead lever: Cure economics.

"Every hour a UV cure line spends waiting on the light train is an hour not spent on the next job. This pair covers your whole UV book of work from one supplier: 9515N-01 for drinking water — styrene-free, FDA-referenced, tensile strength 80–95 MPa — and 9516N-01 for municipal and industrial sewage, with a glass transition temperature of 113°C or higher for a hotter service envelope. One relationship, two SKUs, instead of juggling separate vendors for separate job types."

Proof points: both formulated to be "easy to thicken" (a real operational property — consistent wall buildup during UV cure), both rated for good toughness and high impact strength, and a real field reference: a Guangdong Province job, DN800–DN1000, roughly 5.6 km lined with MERICAN 9516 under UV cure — an actual project, not just a spec claim.

Objection to have an answer ready for: if anyone asks about 9516N-01's flexural modulus, the brochure lists 8.3 GPa — inconsistent with every neighboring figure in the same table (all in the 2.8–4.1 GPa range, including this product's own 3.2 GPa tensile modulus). Don't repeat that number until it's confirmed with Sino Polymer directly; citing a figure that turns out wrong costs more credibility than not having the number at all.
