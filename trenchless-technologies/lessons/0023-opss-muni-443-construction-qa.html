Lesson 23: OPSS.MUNI 443 — Construction & QA

A banned tool, two mandatory camera runs, and a resin-fraud check by infrared spectroscopy — all from the primary document.

Lesson 22 covered design and materials. This is what the spec actually requires on site and in the lab — including one QA mechanism that hasn't come up anywhere else in this workspace: verifying the resin actually used matches what was approved, chemically.

Flow control (443.07.08) — a real redundancy rule

When flow control is required, a primary and standby pump must both be maintained on site, with the standby rated equal to or better than the primary. But the more specific requirement: no pump or related equipment can be disconnected or removed from the site until every service connection has been reinstated and the post-installation video has been recorded. Flow control stays live until the job is fully verified complete — not until the liner is cured.

Preparation (443.07.09) — a banned tool

Flail-type equipment is explicitly prohibited for trimming protruding service laterals that interfere with installation. This is a specific, named exclusion — not a general "use appropriate equipment" clause. If pre-installation inspection reveals an obstruction (a protruding connection, a dropped joint, a collapse) that ordinary cleaning equipment can't clear, a trenchless technique has to be attempted first — excavation requires the Contract Administrator's written approval before it can even start.

Two mandatory CCTV inspections (443.07.10)

Not one — two, at different points in the process, per OPSS 409:

  1. Post-preparation CCTV. Full-length inspection after the pipe is cleaned and prepped, submitted for approval before the 24-hour service interruption notice goes out and before lining installation begins.
  2. Post-lining final CCTV. Full-length inspection after lining is complete, submitted within 48 hours of finishing. The access point walls at each end must be clearly visible on the recording. This inspection specifically checks for: correct fit and finish (no annular space, correctly sized), no stress concentrations (bumps, bubbles, delaminations), a tight fit that reflects the host pipe's actual geometry, no excessive folds or wrinkles, no dry or soft spots, no liner damage, no eruptions or localized defects, and no staining or bleeding on the liner surface.

Curing and cool-down (443.07.12)

Qualified personnel must execute and monitor the cure and keep records — lining temperatures, internal pressure, and UV intensity/duration where applicable — available to the Contract Administrator on request.

  • Heat-cured systems: interface temperature measured and recorded at the downstream and upstream ends, plus any accessible intermediate points, using a thermocouple at the crown and invert. Cool-down follows the manufacturer's recommendations before the hydrostatic head is relieved — done carefully enough to avoid a vacuum that could damage the newly cured liner. Before the curing water is discharged, it has to be cooled to the ambient temperature of the receiving watercourse or sewer — a direct environmental protection requirement.
  • UV-cured systems: the number of lamps, the light train's rate of travel, and total cure duration are measured and recorded, per the manufacturer's specified parameters.

Lateral reinstatement (443.07.16) — a two-stage requirement

Excavation for lateral reinstatement is not allowed — it has to be done internally, with remote-operated equipment (or manually by person-entry where the sewer is large enough). If lateral flow was interrupted by the work, reinstatement has to happen urgently, in two stages: an initial opening sufficient to restore flow, followed by completion to full 100% opening within 24 hours.

Quality assurance (443.08) — the real regime

  1. Post-lining CCTV review. The liner must be continuous with no breaks, separations, thinning, or discontinuity. Any misshape in the liner that doesn't simply reflect the host pipe's actual geometry counts as a deficiency — not a cosmetic issue.
  2. Sample testing. Restrained, field-cured samples are taken from the finished liner at an access point. For liners over 400 mm where a restrained sample isn't feasible, a flat-plate sample is used instead. An independent owner designate manages chain of custody, and testing is done by an independent third-party lab experienced specifically in CIPP testing — not the contractor's own lab.
  3. The bar is the design value, not the code minimum (as flagged in Lesson 22) — test results below the applicable design value make the liner deficient, subject to design reconciliation.
  4. Sampling frequency: 30% of individual liner installations, with at least one tested sample for each liner size installed on the project.
The 15% plate-sample de-rating (443.08.03.02): when a flat-plate sample is used instead of a restrained cylindrical sample, its test results get discounted by 15% before comparing against the required values. Flat-plate samples cure under different conditions than an actual in-pipe restrained sample, so the spec builds in a specific, numeric correction rather than treating the two sample types as equivalent.
Design reconciliation, and its hard limit (443.08.03.03): if a tested property or measured thickness falls short, a design reconciliation — rerunning the original design calculation using the as-tested properties as the only changed input — can be performed with Contract Administrator approval. If the reconciled design still shows adequate performance, the liner isn't deficient. But reconciliation is not permitted at all if flexural strength or flexural modulus comes in below the ASTM F1216 qualifying minimum. You can reconcile down from the design target toward the code floor — never below it.
The resin-verification mechanism (443.08.04) — genuinely novel in this workspace: the testing lab obtains a sample of the approved resin directly from the manufacturer and produces an infrared spectrograph of it — a chemical fingerprint. A sample of the resin actually used in wetout on the job is then sent to a third-party lab, which compares its spectrograph against the manufacturer's reference sample. This is a direct anti-substitution check: it verifies the resin actually installed is chemically the same as what was approved, not just the same product name on a delivery slip.

Spot repairs and lateral liners (443.08.05)

Since access limitations often make it impossible to pull a sample directly from a spot repair or lateral liner, above-ground samples are made instead — from the same materials, on-site, on the same day, using cylindrical forms matching the host pipe size, with procedures (including during cure) designed to keep the sample representative. The same frequency, de-rating, and reconciliation rules apply to these samples as to mainline liner samples.

Construction and QA
