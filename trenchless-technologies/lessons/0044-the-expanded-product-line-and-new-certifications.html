Three real certification pathways, and why they aren't interchangeable

NSF/ANSI 61 is still not confirmed for any specific product — that finding from the certification checklist stands. But three separate, real alternative certifications now show up across the product line, each answering a different question:
  • GB/T17219-1998 — China's own national drinking-water hygiene and safety standard, achieved for MERICAN 3505H and referenced for the 9515N-01/UV-CIPP drinking-water family. A real, legitimate certification — but a Chinese domestic standard, not a substitute for NSF/ANSI 61 on a North American watermain job (Lesson 25's requirement).
  • US FDA food-contact certification (21 CFR 177.2600 / 177.2420) — confirmed via an SGS test report for at least one styrene-free UV-curing product line. This is a real US federal certification, but it's a food-contact-materials pathway, not the water-system-specific NSF/ANSI 61 certification Lesson 25 requires for a watermain liner. The document itself is explicit that NSF 61 is still being "actively pushed," not yet obtained, for at least one product line — an honest, unresolved gap, not a claimed win.
  • TÜV Rheinland (Lesson 43) — mechanics and corrosion-resistance certification, not a drinking-water-safety certification at all. Different purpose entirely.
None of these three is a substitute for NSF/ANSI 61. All three are real, additional, credible evidence of a company that certifies its products through multiple independent bodies — a materially stronger starting position than "some products have passed NSF 61/NSF 51 certification" (the original brochure's unsupported claim, flagged back in Lesson 11) — but the specific NSF/ANSI 61 open item from the certification checklist remains genuinely open.

A regulatory-development credibility signal

Sino Polymer/Huachang is a named participant in drafting T/CECS 1410-2023, a Chinese national technical regulation for CIPP flip-method sewer repair (published by the China Association for Engineering Construction Standardization, effective January 1, 2024), and a separate drafting group for water-supply pipeline laminated-structure repair regulations, referencing GB/T 17219 as the compliance baseline. Photos in the source document show the company's own staff at the drafting committee's founding and review meetings. This doesn't carry direct weight on an Ontario bid — but it's a real, dateable signal that this is an established manufacturer actively shaping standards in its home market, not a peripheral supplier.
