Lesson 70: The Drawings Say “Hot Water or UV”: Cure Method and Material Minimums

Peel’s drawings list the allowed curing method per segment — and it is always both, so the bidder chooses.

A refinement to Lesson 56’s answer to “who decides hot water versus UV”, and a side-by-side of the minimum properties the two Peel specs demand.

Lesson 56 needs an update

Lesson 56 found that Peel’s repair list for the earlier package assigns no cure method to any segment. In this tender the repair summary (Appendix 5.4) again has no cure-method column — but the contract drawings do. The sewer-section table on each plan sheet has a column headed “ALLOWED CURING METHOD”, and on the sheet I viewed (84755-D, segments S5–S10) every row reads “HOT WATER OR UV”. A text search across the plan sheets found the two labels always appearing together, never one alone, so the Region appears to allow both methods on every lined segment. (I viewed one sheet image; the rest is a text tally.)

So the finding sharpens rather than reverses: the Region doesn’t pick for the bidder, it names the allowed set, and the choice is the bidder’s. Item B-5, CIPP Lining, is paid per metre, and the text I read has no separate pay line for UV versus hot water, so cure method is still invisible to the pay items. (The online price forms were not in the folder.)

The two specs compared

Special Provisions 89 and 90 point to Appendix 5.14 (hot water or steam) and Appendix 5.15 (UV). Both are Peel’s standing specifications dated November 2021, and the special provisions override them on any conflict. Minimum in-place properties:

LinerFlexural modulusFlexural strength50-year retention
Hot water or steam, non-reinforced2,400 MPa31 MPaat least 50%
Hot water or steam, reinforced8,000 MPa150 MPaat least 50%
UV (any UV liner)8,000 MPa150 MPaat least 65%

The 2,400 and 8,000 MPa modulus figures match what Lesson 27 recorded for the earlier package (I did not re-compare the other columns). The practical reading: a UV liner has only one performance tier, the high one, and a stricter retention requirement — so UV is not “the same liner, cured faster” in Peel’s paperwork. A non-reinforced hot-water felt liner can use the lower 2,400/31 MPa bar.

Requirements shared by both

  • Design: ASTM F1216-07a Appendix X1, fully deteriorated condition. Both specs say design by later versions of F1216 is not acceptable (compare Lessons 49 and 50 on the editions).
  • Standards named: hot water cites D5813-04(2012), F1216-16, F1743 and F2019; reinforced liners follow F2019-11.
  • Long-term evidence: the product must have had independent long-term (ASTM D2990-17 or equivalent) testing establishing 50-year properties, and a history of successful use in similar-size sewers in Ontario, which the Contract Administrator reviews before award.
  • Resin identity: the Contractor submits the resin manufacturer, type and ID number, and a certified original infrared spectrograph of the catalyzed resin from the manufacturer; an IR check against the baseline may be called for later.
  • Short-term values: must be values the Contractor has reliably and repeatedly obtained in installations of the same liner material.
For the resin thread. Peel’s two long-term requirements — D2990 testing and Ontario installation history — line up with the two gaps Lessons 42–45 tracked for Sino Polymer. This tender names those requirements but also uses a contractor-submitted “CIPP Liners Material Properties Form” (referenced in Item C-7, not in my folder), so the supplier’s data path runs through the contractor, as in Toronto (Lesson 66).
