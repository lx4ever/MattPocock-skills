Lesson 8: Fold-and-Form — Materials, Equipment, Parameters

The simplest materials picture of the three — because there's no resin chemistry to choose between at all.

No resin, no curing agent, no mix ratio. Fold-and-form's material question is really just "which pipe compound and which wall thickness" — closer to specifying a normal pipe than to formulating a coating.

Materials

  • PVC alloy — a purpose-formulated, flexible, ductile PVC compound designed to expand and conform tightly to the host pipe during reversion, without cracking.
  • PE (polyethylene) — the other common option, used where PE's specific properties (e.g. chemical resistance profile) suit the application better.

Wall thickness is typically specified the way ordinary pipe is: a standard schedule (commonly Schedule 40 PVC-equivalent) as the default, with heavier wall thicknesses available by request for jobs with higher structural demands. Diameters typically run in the roughly 4"–26" range for this method.

Sales-relevant point: because there's no resin or cure chemistry, a fold-and-form spec conversation is a completely different shape from a CIPP or SIPP one. You're not asking "which resin, which cure method, what mix ratio" — you're asking "which material (PVC alloy or PE), and what wall thickness/schedule." If a fold-and-form proposal mentions a resin or a cure time, that's not a variant — it's a sign the term is being used loosely, or possibly wrongly, for what's actually a CIPP or SIPP job.

Equipment

  1. Steam conditioning cabinet. Softens the folded, coiled liner before it's pulled into the pipe.
  2. Winch and cable. Pulls the softened liner through the host pipe from one manhole to another.
  3. Air compressor. Supplies the pressure used alongside steam during reversion, to push the liner back round and snug against the host wall.
  4. Cooling supply. Compressed air or a water-air mix, used to cool and set the liner's new round shape.
  5. Robotic cutter. Trims the liner ends and reopens laterals — the same category of tool used across all three methods for this final step.

Parameters worth knowing

ParameterTypical figure
Softening / reversion steam temperatureCommonly cited around 125°C
Cool-downUntil exhaust temperature holds steady around 110°F, for roughly 20 minutes
Liner undersizingManufactured about 2–2.5% smaller than the host pipe's inside diameter, on purpose
Trim overhangRoughly 75–150 mm left protruding at each access point, to preserve the tight fit
Lateral opening toleranceRoughly 90–105% of the original connection's diameter, smooth and circular
Maximum practical run lengthContinuous installs up to roughly 1,500 linear feet have been done in a single pull
