Lesson 75: Where It’s Made: Plants, Country of Origin, and the Tender Forms

Four partner plants cover Europe, the Middle East, South Asia and Southeast Asia, none covers North America, and both tenders now ask where the resin was made.

Lesson 64 and 69 showed tender forms that record a resin’s origin. Here is what Sino Polymer’s own documents say about where its resin is made.

The plants the profile names

The profile’s “Based in China, serving the world” slide lists four partner companies under “localized production” (the slide doesn’t say who owns them):

No localized plant covers North America. The deck mentions North American distributors, but nowhere that a CIPP resin is made outside Shanghai or that any production or blending exists in Canada. Everything below is conditional on that.

Why origin is now a form field

TenderWhere resin origin is asked
Toronto 27TW-CPI-05CWDPricing Form: “Country of Origin” of goods, defined as the last country in which the goods were produced (Lesson 64); Form F lists CIPP Resin as a Major Good in Ontario / Canadian / Other columns
Peel 2026-323TDomestic Supply Chain Plan from the lowest-priced compliant bidder; 51% floor; plus a tariff-impact table and a clause on later tariffs (Lesson 69)

If a resin were produced entirely in Shanghai, both tenders would record it as China. Produced at a partner plant in Italy or Malaysia, the “last country in which produced” would be that country. That could change the tariff exposure Peel asks about, since tariff rules depend on origin — but I don’t know which tariffs apply to which origin, and neither tender says. It would not make the resin “Canadian-made”, which needs 51% of direct costs in Canada or a “Made in Canada” label (Lesson 64).

The one explicit shelf-life figure

Lessons 59 and 60 left open how much shelf life a multi-week Shanghai-to-Ontario crossing uses up. The only shelf-life number in these two documents is for MFE 711, a bisphenol-A vinyl ester sold for FRP pipes and tanks: “shell [sic] life 10 months” in both the profile and the catalogue. That is a general-purpose product, not one of the promoted CIPP resins, and it is not an installed-in-Ontario number. Nothing in either document gives shelf life for 9405T, 9515 or any other CIPP resin, so the open question stands.

Questions this raises for a contractor conversation: Is the resin it would buy made in Shanghai or at a partner plant? What origin would it declare? How is it pricing duties and any surtaxes into a tariff-impact answer (Peel) or the Pricing Form (Toronto)? What shelf life remains on arrival?
