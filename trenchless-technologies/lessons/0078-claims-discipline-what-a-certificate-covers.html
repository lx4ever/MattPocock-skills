Lesson 78: Claims Discipline: What Each Certificate Actually Covers

Seven rows on one slide, seven different scopes — and none of them is what a municipal sewer tender asks a resin maker for.

The later deck’s own notes teach how to read a certification claim. Applied row by row, they show what each one does and doesn’t establish.

One slide, seven different kinds of evidence

The 0930 deck’s “Quality and Certifications” slide is headed “Certified for specific products and uses”. Its speaker note adds: “There are two kinds of evidence on this slide. The ISO systems concern company management. The other rows concern particular products, formulations, specimens, or applications.” Row by row, with what I could find in the documents read:

Three questions to ask of any claim

  1. Which exact grade? A fire result for one formulation cannot be extended to its family (the note says so).
  2. Which standard, which edition, which scope? “Food-contact” is not “drinking-water approval”; a Chinese hygiene standard is not an Ontario listing.
  3. Who issued it, when, and on what specimen and conditions? Certification, test result and compliance statement are three different things.

What the two sewer tenders actually ask for

None of the rows above is something Toronto’s 27TW-CPI-05CWD or Peel’s 2026-323T asks a sewer-lining resin to hold. Those tenders ask for data and history: Form E flexural values with five accredited-lab field-sample reports (Lesson 66), a certified infrared spectrograph, independent D2990 long-term testing and Ontario installation history (Lesson 70). The certifications on this slide matter for other markets, and the drinking-water rows matter for watermain work, not for the sanitary and combined sewers in these two tenders. So the strongest sewer credential is not on the slide; it is the evidence Lessons 66 and 70 list.

This is the same discipline as Lesson 18’s correction: a confident-sounding label (there, a standard number) is only as good as the primary source behind it. The 0930 deck’s presenter checks show the company applying that discipline to itself.
