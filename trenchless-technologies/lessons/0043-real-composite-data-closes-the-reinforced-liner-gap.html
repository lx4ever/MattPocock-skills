Lesson 43: Real Composite Data Closes the Reinforced-Liner Gap

The single largest open item on the certification checklist, repeated in Lessons 27, 39, and 41: every Sino Polymer number seen so far was neat resin, not the cured composite Peel's reinforced/UV bar actually tests. That's no longer true.

The headline finding: the newly reviewed technical deck includes actual cured-composite test data for UV-CIPP resin hose products — not cast neat-resin splines. Two separate composite test results:
Product/testTensile strengthTensile modulusFlexural strengthFlexural modulus
UV-CIPP hose (MERICAN 9515N family)520 MPa25 GPa (25,000 MPa)460 MPa16 GPa (16,000 MPa)
Water-supply UV-CIPP styrene-free hose435 MPa24.8 GPa580 MPa19.8 GPa (19,800 MPa)

Both tested per GB/T 1447-2005 (China's standard tensile/flexural test method for fiber-reinforced plastics — the composite equivalent of ASTM D638/D790), on the actual cured, fiberglass-reinforced hose product, not a cast resin sample.

Checked directly against the bar this was always about

Peel Region's reinforced/UV liner minimum is 8,000 MPa flexural modulus (Lesson 27). Both composite results above — 16,000 MPa and 19,800 MPa — clear that bar by a factor of two to two-and-a-half. This is the exact comparison the certification checklist has been waiting to make since Lesson 27 first established that Sino Polymer's neat-resin data "says essentially nothing about whether a fiberglass-reinforced composite using their resin would clear 8,000 MPa." Now there's a real answer, and it's a strong one.

What this evidence is, precisely — and what it isn't yet

Being precise about scope matters here, the same way it mattered when correcting the OPSS.MUNI citation error earlier in this workspace:

  • What it is: real, GB/T-standard-tested, cured composite hose data, from Sino Polymer's own reported figures, for at least two named UV-CIPP hose product lines. This directly demonstrates the resin-plus-fiberglass system — not just the neat resin — performs well above the reinforced-liner bar.
  • What it isn't yet: a test run at the exact resin-to-fiber ratio and tube construction a specific contractor would use on a specific Peel Region job (Lesson 41's Step 3), tested to the specific ASTM D790/ISO 11296 methods Peel's spec names, by an independent third-party lab the way Paragon Systems tests real Peel Region samples (Lesson 26). GB/T 1447-2005 is a real, legitimate Chinese national standard — but it isn't the same document ASTM D790 or ISO 11296 is, the same distinction flagged for the ISO 10468/ASTM D2990 creep data in Lesson 42.
The practical upshot: this is no longer an open question of "does a composite made from this resin even come close" — it demonstrably does, with real margin. What remains is a narrower, much more tractable question: getting a specific composite tested to the specific named standard, at the specific job's construction, by a specific accepted lab. That's a real but much smaller step than starting from nothing, which was the certification checklist's original framing.

A third-party certification worth naming directly

The same source states Sino Polymer's UV-curing resin has "completed more than 3,000 tons of resin application cases in the field of CIPP light-curing repair hoses, and has passed the certification of mechanics and corrosion resistance of TÜV Rheinland" — with actual test report images shown (a resin structure test report and a corrosion resistance test report, both TÜV-branded). TÜV Rheinland is a large, internationally recognized independent testing and certification body headquartered in Germany, with real global standing — this is a genuinely different class of evidence than the brochure's earlier qualitative claims ("excellent corrosion resistance") that Lesson 11 and the certification checklist flagged as unsupported by a named test report. This one has a name, a report, and international credibility behind it.
