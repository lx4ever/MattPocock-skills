Lesson 77: Two September 30 Decks: What Changed Between Them

The later deck corrects its draft against the company profile: lower capacity, sourced flash-point language, no unsupported supply-security claim.

Two presentations built from the same materials differ in ways worth learning to spot, especially where a claim feeds a tender form.

The two decks

Both are Sino Polymer presentations from September 30: “CAMX speech” (11 slides, no speaker notes) and “Sino Polymer Presentation 0930” (17 slides, with speaker notes). The 0930 deck reuses the CAMX artwork but its notes cite the company profile page by page and end with “Presenter checks” — instructions to the speaker about what must be verified before customer use. Reading the two side by side shows a draft being tightened against its source.

Two of these tie straight back to this workspace. “Non-flammable” was dropped because a flash point of 110 °C means the resin is combustible but falls outside the flammable-liquid transport class (Lesson 46’s flash-point math). And the capacity and footprint changes update Lessons 74 and 75, which now carry notes saying so.

Three checks I could run against the catalogue

  1. Cure times. The 0930 slide groups MERICAN 3314, 3316 and 3300 as “3–5 min demould at 130–150 °C”. The catalogue (read by eye) gives 3–5 minutes for 3316 only, 8–10 minutes for 3314, and no cure time for 3300. The speaker note itself flags that the cycle-time detail came from a catalogue that had not been reviewed.
  2. UL 94 V-0. The presenter check says the profile does not substantiate every listed grade. Between the profile and catalogue, each of MERICAN 3313-3, 3314, 3315 and 3316 does carry a UL 94 V-0 statement — so the claim exists for all four, but a statement in marketing material is not a UL listing.
  3. ASTM E84 Class A. The slide lists MFE 707H and 707A together. The catalogue says 707A reaches Class A “with 5% Sb₂O₃ added”, a condition the slide drops.

The CIPP slide, which did not change

Both decks give the same CIPP map: spot repair (MERICAN 116A/B, two-component polyurethane), hot water or steam inversion (9405T, 9602; MFE 11T, 770T) and UV (9515, 9516; MFE 775UV, 752UV), with municipal wastewater at 50–60 °C and industrial up to 100 °C. The 0930 deck adds supply volumes: 9405T over 1,000 t and UV resins over 3,000 t, which come from the larger technical deck already in this workspace (Lesson 45). The period and places aren’t stated, so they show scale, not Ontario history — and against Lesson 74’s estimate of at most about 80 t for the whole Peel job, they are large.
