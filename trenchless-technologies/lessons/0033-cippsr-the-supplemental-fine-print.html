Lesson 33: CIPPSR — The Supplemental Fine Print

Lesson 26 covered Appendix 5.11, the base CIPP spot-repair spec. This document amends it — the same base-plus-supplement pattern already seen with 5.9/5.10 and OPSS.MUNI 100/Peel's General Conditions, applied one more time.

Six clauses (SSS.02 through SSS.08), each changing a specific piece of who-pays-for-what or how-much-time-you-get on a CIPPSR job. None of it changes the physical spot-repair spec from Lesson 26 — thickness minimums, cure requirements, odour control are all untouched. This is entirely about risk allocation and scheduling.

CCTV data: useful, but not a guarantee

The Contractor bears the risk of relying on the Agency's provided CCTV survey. If field conditions turn out to differ from what the CCTV showed, no additional payment is owed — unless the difference is "material and significant" versus the baseline condition documented at bid time. This is a real risk-allocation clause: existing inspection data is a starting point, not a warranty of actual field conditions.

Flow control moved from optional to mandatory-and-included

In the base spec, bypass pumping and flow control were a Provisional item — priced separately, used only if actually needed. This supplemental spec reclassifies flow control as Base Scope: included in the bid price for flows up to 63 L/s (about 1,000 US gpm), with no separate line-item payment. If full bypass pumping is required, the Contractor must submit a Bypass Management Plan two weeks in advance, including spill-prevention measures and the Ministry of Environment notification procedure for any accidental discharge.

Why this matters for a quote: a bid item that used to be billed separately, when actually triggered, is now baked into the base price whether or not it's needed. That changes the risk profile of the base bid price itself — a contractor pricing this line now has to account for potential flow-control costs whether or not the job ends up needing them.

Real time allowances, not vague scheduling language

  • Cleaning and prep: the entire pipe length gets cleaned, not just the immediate repair location. Where no prior CCTV survey exists for a segment, the Contractor gets a baseline allowance of 2 full working days (20 hours) for reaming, cutting roots, and flushing before extra time becomes billable.
  • Protruding lateral trimming is incidental to the CIPPSR price — no separate payment for it. Robotic cutters are the approved trimming method; flailing tools and chain knockers are explicitly prohibited (they risk damaging the host pipe or an adjacent liner).
  • Void filling behind the host pipe is required before lining proceeds — unless the void is large enough that open-cut repair becomes the better option instead of lining over it. That's a real judgment call built into the spec, not a rigid rule.

Design efficiency: one stamped design can cover a range

The Contractor provides the P.Eng.-stamped liner design after award, not before bid — based on a Base Bid Design Parameters table the Agency supplies. A single stamped design is allowed to cover a range of burial depths for a given pipe size, rather than requiring a separate calculation for every individual repair location. This is a real efficiency provision: it means engineering cost doesn't scale linearly with the number of individual spot repairs on a contract.

Sample testing: a real deadline with teeth

Same testing agency as Lesson 26 (Paragon Systems, Concord ON), with a hard 10-business-day turnaround requirement for test reports after installation. If that deadline isn't met, the spec allows the Agency to suspend further work until reports catch up — a real schedule-risk clause, not just a target. Sampling and testing costs themselves are incidental to the CIPPSR price — no separate payment line for them.
