Lesson 67: Reading This Repair List and Contract: Blind Shots, a Rail Crossing, and the Payment Terms

Six awkward segments, a siphon and a payment schedule explain most of what a contractor will worry about — and what a resin supplier’s paperwork releases.

Lesson 35 read Peel’s repair list line by line. Here is the same exercise for Toronto’s shorter list, with the contract terms that decide when a contractor actually gets paid.

The unusual segments

Most of the 37 lined segments are routine. Six are not (plus a non-lined siphon), and the spec says plainly that any extra effort for them is included in the unit price:

ItemWhat is different
B.01.04 (SL1445954)Transition liner where brick (about 550 mm) meets PVC (about 525 mm); the Contractor confirms actual dimensions before lining
B.02.03, B.02.06, B.02.07, B.04.19Blind shots — lined from one manhole only. Ends are terminated by man-entry into a 2700 mm sewer (B.02.06), by robotic cutting, or, for B.02.07, with pressure control so a brick wall closing the old junction isn’t damaged
B.03.04 (SL1447484)Rail corridor. Bypass piping may not cross the railway, so flow (estimated peak dry-weather 55 L/s) must be diverted along a roughly 210 m route on Sousa Mendes St with buried road crossings and driveway ramps
C.01.01 / F.01.20 (SL1445812)A 300 mm siphon sewer: V1 inspection plus flushing and debris removal, with the lining itself provisional

Twenty rows of the list also carry a coordination flag for Hydro One overhead and underground civil and electrical work, and one for a TTC infrastructure project. These are “items for information” — incidental to the work and not separately paid.

A small lesson in reading documents. The spec and the repair list disagree in places. The spec gives B.02.03 as 13.5 m, B.02.06 as 9.0 m and the B.01.04 transition as starting 78 m from the manhole, while the sheet shows 13.2 m, 8.7 m and a 99.5 m segment. The spec’s B.02.07 text names junction JP3551008413, which the sheet assigns to B.02.03, not B.02.07. Lining is paid on actual field length (manhole edge to manhole edge), so the length gaps matter little — but the node mismatch is worth a question before the deadline. Lesson 36 made the same point about trusting no single table.

Contract terms that shape cash flow

TermThis tender
Payment for lining5% on accepted V1 • 25% on pipe preparation and accepted V2 • 50% on liner installed and accepted V3 • 20% on accepted QA/QC (sample test report, wet-out sheet, curing log)
Delay liquidated damages$3,500 per calendar day
Holdback3% contractual holdback from each proper invoice, on top of the statutory 10% lien holdback defined in the agreement
Warranty2 years from Substantial Performance
Schedule150 Working Days; installation procedure due within 10 working days of the Commencement Date; blind-shot procedures at least 10 days before installation; MH-modification work plans at least 30 days before work
Unit-price completenessThe pricing instructions void a bid with blank or unclear cells (“n/a”, “included”, “tba”) and forbid pricing assumptions or contingencies

The 20% held back until QA/QC paperwork is accepted is why the resin submittals and lab results from Lesson 66 are a cash-flow matter for the contractor, not only a compliance one. The milestone split is identical to the 2025 tender (Lesson 55); this contract is shorter, at 150 Working Days.
