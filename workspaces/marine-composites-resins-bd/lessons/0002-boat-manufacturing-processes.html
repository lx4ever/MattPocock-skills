How a Fiberglass Boat Hull Actually Gets Built

This is the first of the boats deep-dive. Before you can sell resin to a boat builder, you need to know which of three processes their plant runs — because it changes what properties of your resin they actually care about.

Why this is the first boats lesson

"We sell resin" isn't a pitch. "Your infusion line needs resin with the flow characteristics and pot life to fill a 40-foot hull before it gels" is a pitch — but only if it's true of that specific builder. You can't tailor anything until you know how their hull leaves the mold.

Three ways a hull gets built

ProcessHow it worksWhat matters about the resin
Hand layup (open mold) Worker brushes/rolls resin onto dry fiberglass layer by layer, in an open mold, by hand.1 Workable pot life, easy wet-out by hand, styrene release is uncontrolled — this is the process EPA/state regulators target hardest (see Lesson 3).
Vacuum infusion (a closed-mold process) Dry fiberglass is laid in the mold, sealed under a vacuum bag, and resin is pulled through it by vacuum pressure through flow media.2 Low viscosity and controlled gel time are critical — resin has to flow through the entire laminate stack before it starts curing, or the pour fails mid-hull. Yields a more predictable, typically higher-glass-content laminate (resin and glass roughly 50/50 by weight) than hand layup.2
Resin transfer molding (RTM) (also closed-mold) Similar to infusion, but resin is pumped in under pressure rather than pulled by vacuum, using a heavier two-part (male + female) mold.3 Similar flow/gel-time requirements to infusion, plus needs to tolerate injection pressure. Higher tooling cost limits this to higher-volume or higher-precision builders.

Sources: 1 CompositesWorld, "Fabrication Methods" · 2 Power & Motor Yacht, "Vacuum Resin Infusion Explained" · 3 Wikipedia, "Resin transfer moulding".

The BD angle. "Closed mold" (infusion and RTM together) is the industry's answer to two pressures at once: tighter styrene-emissions regulation, and the drive for lighter, more consistent hulls. A builder still running open-mold hand layup is either small-volume/custom, or hasn't made the capital investment yet — that's a different conversation (resin as a cost/compliance lever) than a builder who's already infusing (resin as a process-performance lever). Lesson 3 covers the regulatory side in detail.

Quick recall check

One word each.

1. Brushing resin onto dry fiberglass in an open mold, by hand, is called ______ layup.

2. Pulling resin through dry fabric with vacuum pressure in a sealed bag is called resin ______.

3. Pumping resin in under pressure using a two-part mold (no vacuum) is resin transfer ______.

4. Infusion and RTM are both examples of ______ molding, as opposed to open-mold hand layup.
