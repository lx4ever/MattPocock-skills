Resin Comparison Cheat Sheet

The compressed version of Lesson 0001. Print this, keep it in the car.

Family Cures via Relative cost Water/blister resistance Strength & flexibility Typical marine use today
Polyester Styrene + catalyst (e.g. MEKP) $ — lowest Weakest — prone to osmotic blistering over years of water immersion Adequate; more brittle than the other two Structural/bulk layup in most production fiberglass boat hulls; ceramic-infused polyester used as an added core layer in some pool shells
Vinyl ester Styrene + catalyst, tighter molecular backbone than polyester $$ — middle Much better than polyester — the standard fix for blister-prone hulls and shells Better toughness/flex than polyester Skin coat (outer laminate layer) on many boat hulls; full-laminate standard for premium fiberglass pool shells
Epoxy Resin + hardener (no styrene) $$$ — highest Excellent — generally considered blister-free Best strength-to-weight; most durable bond to core/wood High-performance and repair work (wood/epoxy composite boats, structural repairs); composite (non-rotomolded) kayak hulls

Segment quick-reference (landscape view — see Lesson 2 for depth)

SegmentDominant material/process today
Boats (production fiberglass)Polyester structural layup + vinyl ester skin coat
PWC (Sea-Doo, Yamaha)Increasingly proprietary molded compounds — SMC, Sea-Doo Polytec (glass fiber/polypropylene blend), Yamaha NanoXcel — rather than open resin/mat layup
Kayak/canoeSplit market: rotomolded HDPE (no thermoset resin) at the value end; fiberglass/Kevlar/carbon with epoxy or polyester at the performance end; thermoformed sheet plastic in between
Pools (fiberglass inground shells)Vinyl ester throughout the laminate, specifically to prevent underwater osmotic blistering
