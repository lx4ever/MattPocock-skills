Canadian Boat Building Standards Cheat Sheet

The compressed version of Lesson 0004.

ItemWhat it isResin relevance
Small Vessel Regulations (SOR/2010-91) Federal regulation under the Canada Shipping Act, 2001, governing vessels up to 15 gross tonnage Points to TP 1332 as the baseline construction standard
TP 1332 Transport Canada's "Construction Standards for Small Vessels" (2010 edition) Unverified claim, do not repeat to a customer: a single search snippet suggests it requires flame-retardant additives in the exposed resin layer within the engine space (or woven roving as the final lay-up layer there). Could not be checked against the source PDF — see below.
ABYC alternative Transport Canada's "TIER I – Policy: Acceptance of Alternative Construction Requirements for Small Vessels," effective Oct 30, 2019 (corroborated by 3+ independent sources, incl. ABYC's own Craig Scholten quote) Two tiers: Appendix 1 (Tables 1–2) covers pleasure craft <24m + non-pleasure ≤6m; Appendix 2 (Tables 3–4) covers non-pleasure >6m. Named standards seen in search results (H-24 fuel, H-32 ventilation, E-11 electrical, H-27 thru-hulls) are systems/equipment standards, not laminate or resin specs — unverified single-source claim, but if accurate, resin/laminate spec stays under TP 1332 directly, not ABYC.
Federal VOC/styrene emissions regulation Unlike the US EPA's boat-manufacturing NESHAP (Lesson 0003), no equivalent Canada-wide, boat-specific rule was confirmed in this workspace's research Gap — verify before relying on this in a pitch. Don't assume the US "compliance risk" resin pitch transfers to a Canadian builder as-is.

Practical takeaway. Lead with TP 1332 / ABYC when talking construction compliance with a Canadian builder — the ABYC-alternative policy's existence, title, date, and two-tier structure are well corroborated. But don't pitch "ABYC compliance" as your entry point for a resin/laminate conversation — ABYC's accepted standards under this policy look to be systems/equipment standards (fuel, electrical, ventilation, fittings), not structural laminate specs. TP 1332 itself is where resin/laminate requirements would live, and the one specific claim found there (flame-retardant resin in the engine space) is not yet confirmed — useful as a question to ask a builder, not as a fact to assert. Don't lead with an emissions-compliance angle the way you might with a US builder; verify provincial air-quality rules for the specific region first.
