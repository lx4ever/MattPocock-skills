# Mission: Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

## Why
You do business development at Kanata Electronics and need to talk credibly about the Canadian aerospace, space and defence market — who buys, who builds, which programs are live right now, and how a Tier 2/3 electronics supplier actually gets into a prime's supply chain — to identify and pursue real opportunities.

## Success looks like
- You can name the three federal bodies that shape a defence buy (DND, PSPC, ISED) and say in one sentence what each one actually controls.
- You can explain the Industrial and Technological Benefits (ITB) Value Proposition well enough to know *why* a prime would subcontract to a Canadian SME at all — it's not goodwill, it's a contractual obligation scored in the bid.
- You can name and place Canada's current anchor defence platform programs (River-class Destroyer, Patrol Submarine, AOPS, JSS, P-8A/CMMA, F-35, RPAS, CC-330, FAcT, MSMM, MDA CHORUS) — which service each belongs to, and roughly what stage each is at.
- You can read a program's status and translate it into a BD question: is this program still in competition (chase the prime's ITB Value Proposition), in early build (chase Tier 1/2 supply chain onboarding), or in sustainment (chase in-service support subcontracts)?

## Constraints
- No prior defence-industry or government-procurement background — start from zero, no assumed jargon.
- BD context — prioritize "who to call and why" over deep technical/engineering detail on the platforms themselves.
- Lessons should be short, each giving one usable, tangible win.

## Out of scope
- Deep technical/engineering specifications of the platforms themselves (radar systems, hull design, etc.) — useful only if a specific pursuit requires it later.
- U.S. or allied defence procurement systems (FMS, DFARS) — separate mission if it comes up.
