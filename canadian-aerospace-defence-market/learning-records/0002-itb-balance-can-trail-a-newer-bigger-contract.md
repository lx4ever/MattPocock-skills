# 0002: A "$0 to be identified" ITB balance can mean the register hasn't caught up to a newer, bigger contract — not that the opportunity is gone

**From:** Lesson 3 (River-class Destroyer)

## The finding

River-class Destroyer's ISED-registered ITB obligation (~$937M) shows $0 "to be identified" — fully committed. Taken alone, that reads as "no open Canadian-content balance to chase." But that $937M figure is the 2019 **Definition** contract only. The **construction** contract — $8B CAD for the first three ships alone, awarded March 2025, with 12 more hulls still to come — is a separate, much larger award whose own ITB obligation isn't yet broken out on the register the same way.

Kanata's own Pipeline sheet doesn't agree with the ITB balance column for this program: 29 tracked opportunities, top score 27 (the highest of any program reviewed so far in this workspace), built from live news signals rather than the ISED dollar balance. A real, dated proof point backs this up directly: Irving Shipbuilding awarded Ottawa-based W.R. Davis Engineering a $30M+ subcontract in July 2024, well after the Definition contract had already been substantially spent.

## Why it matters

This is the same underlying fact as Lesson 2's finding (CMMA showing "$0 completed" despite a real, announced $61M investment) wearing a different symptom: the ISED obligations register is an accounting snapshot tied to specific, individually-registered contracts, not a live read on a program's total opportunity. On a program with multiple contract phases (Definition, then Construction), a "closed-looking" balance on an early contract says nothing about the obligation on a newer, larger one that just hasn't been separately registered yet.

## Follow-up

When sizing any program's current opportunity, check whether the ISED-registered project is the current active contract phase or an earlier one — and cross-check against the Pipeline sheet's live score/opportunity count, which seems to be a better real-time signal than the ITB balance column when the two disagree this sharply.
