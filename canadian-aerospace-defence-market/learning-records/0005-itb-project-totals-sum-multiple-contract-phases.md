# 0005: A single ITB "Project" total can be the sum of several sequential contracts, not one award

**From:** Lesson 6 (Canadarm3)

## The finding

Kanata's tracker shows Canadarm3's ITB "Contract Award Date" as 2022 and total obligation as $1,319,983,365. Neither figure describes the program's biggest, most recent contract on its own: 2022 was Phase B ($269M, preliminary design); the largest award, Phase C/D ($1B, final design through manufacturing/test), landed in June 2024. The two contract values alone (~$1.27B) already account for nearly all of the registered $1.32B total — ISED tracks the whole "Gateway External Robotics Systems-Canadarm3" project under one obligation figure that grows as each sequential phase (A, B, C/D, and presumably future in-service phases) gets awarded and registered.

## Why it matters

This is a variant of the pattern from Lessons 2, 3, and 5: an ITB register figure describes an accounting object (here, a single registered "Project" spanning a multi-phase program), not a single news event. The award date and total obligation shown will reflect whichever phase most recently updated the registration — not necessarily the most recent or largest contract news. Before reading an ITB figure as "the deal," check whether the program has a public multi-phase contract history (A/B/C/D-style, or Definition/Construction-style as in River-class Destroyer) that the single registered number is quietly summing.

## Follow-up

Worth checking whether other multi-phase programs in Kanata's curated list (RADARSAT/CHORUS, Telesat Lightspeed) show the same pattern — a single ITB total masking several sequential contract awards — before treating any one program's registered figure as a single clean transaction.
