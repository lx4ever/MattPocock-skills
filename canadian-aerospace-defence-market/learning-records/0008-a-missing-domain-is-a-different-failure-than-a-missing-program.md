# 0008: A domain that's entirely absent from the curated list is a different, more basic problem than one missing program within a populated domain

**From:** Lesson 9 (Land and Training domains)

## The finding

Checking whether the patterns found across Naval, Air, and Space (Lessons 1–8) hold in Land and Training turned up something bigger than expected. Training is thin — only FAcT is curated, with a few small untied items (CAE's C130J/CH-147 training-systems contracts) left over. Land is not thin, it's **entirely absent**: zero programs, zero tier, zero Pipeline entries, across a domain where the ITB Obligations sheet shows roughly $2.71B in real, currently-open "to be identified" money spread across six named contractors (General Dynamics Land Systems Canada, General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada, Rheinmetall Canada, Saab Canada, KNDS Deutschland, GM Defense Canada) — and that's before counting the further Land rows sitting at $0 "to be identified."

The "hub" pattern from Lesson 5 (MDA concentrating space-domain relationships) still holds here in a different guise: General Dynamics (its Land Systems and Mission Systems-Canada entities together) and Rheinmetall Canada account for most of the Land rows. But the "maturity vs. balance mismatch" pattern from Lesson 7 can't be tested at all for Land — there's no pipeline score to compare the balance against, because there's no curated program row to hang one on.

## Why it matters

This reframes what "gap-checking" a BD tracker actually means. A missing *program* (RPAS, Lesson 8) is a curation backlog — the data exists, it just hasn't been promoted. A missing *domain* (Land) suggests the original curation effort's scope may never have included it at all, which isn't fixable by adding one row — it needs a deliberate decision about whether Land is in scope for Kanata's pursuit strategy, and if so, a first pass at building out that domain's tier structure from scratch.

## Follow-up

Worth asking directly (not assuming) whether Land was excluded on purpose — Kanata's actual manufacturing capability may not fit land-vehicle or C4ISR work the way it fits harness/electronics scope on ships, aircraft, and spacecraft — before treating this as something to fix.
