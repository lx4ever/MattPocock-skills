# 0019: Not every named program is an open door — some are already closed, and the real lead is one level down

**From:** Lesson 20 (UxS DISH / NEXUS)

## The finding

NEXUS looked, on first mention, like it might be another DDI-style standing application (Lesson 16). It isn't: the consortium was selected through a Call for Proposals that closed April 2, 2026, and the 30 selected members are the hub's primary users going forward. There's no application for Kanata to make here. But one named member, Octasic, has a stated focus (RF/SDR, spectrum sensing, counter-drone payloads) that's a closer match to Kanata's harness/electrical-assembly capability than the hub's general C-UAS/counter-robotics scope. The real lead isn't the program — it's a specific company inside it.

## Why it matters

Worth checking, for any newly-surfaced program, whether it's actually an open application (like DDI) or a closed consortium (like NEXUS) before spending effort on a pursuit strategy aimed at the program itself. When it's closed, the useful move is scanning the named members for the closest capability match and treating that company as the actual lead — not the program as a whole.

## Follow-up

Check whether Octasic has any public supplier/partner engagement process before deciding whether this lead is worth active pursuit or just worth watching.
