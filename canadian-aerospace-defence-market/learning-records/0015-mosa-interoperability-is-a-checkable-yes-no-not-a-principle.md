# 0015: MOSA / interoperability requirements on a real application are specific yes/no facts, not abstract principles

**From:** Lesson 16 (DDI Marketplace, MOSA & Interoperability)

## The finding

MOSA (Modular Open Systems Approach) sounds like acquisition-strategy theory until it shows up on an actual application. DDI's Technology Questionnaire turns it into four concrete, checkable questions: does the system use open interfaces and MOSA; can Canada integrate third-party payloads/radios/sensors/batteries/software into it; do interface control documents (ICDs) exist; can it operate without vendor cloud services. None of these are judgment calls — each has a factual yes/no answer about a specific product.

## Why it matters

This reframes what "MOSA-compliant" or "interoperable" means for Kanata's actual positioning: not a philosophy to agree with, but a documentation and design-practice question — do our harness/connector designs use standard, documented interfaces rather than one-off proprietary ones, and can we produce an ICD for a given assembly on request. That's answerable now, concretely, rather than something to research further in the abstract.

## Follow-up

Worth checking, before drafting DDI's Stream 1 application, whether Kanata's existing harness/connector designs are already documented to ICD standard for any past program, or whether that documentation would need to be produced specifically for this application — directly affects how fast a credible "yes" can be given on the questionnaire.
