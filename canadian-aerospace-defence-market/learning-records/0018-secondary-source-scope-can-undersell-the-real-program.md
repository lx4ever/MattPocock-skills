# 0018: A program built from secondary sources can quietly undersell its real scope — worth revisiting once primary material shows up

**From:** Lesson 19 (CAF UxS Enterprise Model)

## The finding

Lesson 16 was built entirely from news coverage and search-derived summaries of the DDI Marketplace, and described Stream 1 as "uncrewed and counter-uncrewed systems and components" — which reads as aerial-drone-focused, and was interpreted that way throughout Lessons 16–18. Official CAF/DIA briefing material (photographed conference slides, no public URL) names that same stream "Hardware" and lists it explicitly as UAS, UGV, USV, UUV, and attritable systems — ground and maritime uncrewed vehicles included, not just aerial. The secondary-source version wasn't wrong, exactly — it just compressed a five-category hardware stream down to the one category ("drones") that dominates news coverage of the program.

The same gap showed up twice more in the same lesson: the mechanism after qualification (persistent, competitive mini-solicitations — not explained in any secondary source found) and the actual five MOSA requirements plus an explicit IP-retention reassurance, both far more precise than what general MOSA background research produced.

## Why it matters

News coverage of a new government program tends to compress toward whatever's most newsworthy (drones), even when the program's actual scope is broader. When a lesson is built entirely from secondary sources on a fast-moving new initiative, it's worth treating that lesson's scope as provisional — narrower than reality by default — until primary material (official briefings, the program's own documentation) becomes available to check it against.

## Follow-up

Worth revisiting other lessons built primarily from search-derived summaries (Lesson 17's CAF UAS landscape, Lesson 18's DIA/Strix/FedDev) the same way if primary material for those ever surfaces — the DDI correction here may not be a one-off.
