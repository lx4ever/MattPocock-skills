# 0025: Something covered twice as background context can turn out to be the single most actionable door found so far

**From:** Lesson 24 (Team SkyGuardian Canada)

## The finding

Team SkyGuardian Canada appeared in Lessons 8 and 15 purely as context — "the industry team behind RPAS," cited once for its four named members and their individual contracts. Neither lesson checked whether it was actually an open door. It is, more directly than almost anything else in this workspace: over 2,000 Canadian suppliers already engaged, an explicit, named, standing application (ga-asi.com/TeamSkyGuardianCanada, not a competitive window or a closed consortium), GA-ASI stating it's "particularly" seeking small and medium businesses, a named fit category ("global supply chain for aircraft components and manufacturing") that matches Kanata directly, and an office in Ottawa.

Compare this to the other doors catalogued recently: DDI is competitive and deadline-driven (Lesson 16), CDIR is right-stage-wrong-wave (Lesson 23), CDDE is indirect and university-mediated (Lesson 23). Team SkyGuardian is none of those complications — it's closer to "just apply."

## Why it matters

A fact cited as supporting context for one finding (here: "who's on this program's industry team") is worth a second look on its own terms before assuming it's fully covered. The most useful lead in this lesson wasn't new information about the program — it was asking a question the earlier lessons never asked: is there actually a way in, and how open is it.

## Follow-up

This is the strongest single "apply now" candidate surfaced in the workspace so far — worth prioritizing a real application draft here before some of the more complicated, timing- or competition-gated leads (CDIR, DDIM, Maritime DISH).
