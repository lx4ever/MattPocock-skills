# 0003: A program with zero registered ITB is often the *most* time-sensitive pursuit, not the least

**From:** Lesson 4 (Canadian Patrol Submarine Project)

## The finding

CPSP has no ITB obligation on the ISED register at all — the contract with TKMS hasn't been signed yet (preferred supplier only, as of July 2026; contract to conclude by end of 2027). That could easily read as "nothing to chase here yet." In fact the opposite is true: TKMS has already stood up a formal mechanism (the Canadian Defence and Dual-Use Innovation Ecosystem, CDDE) to pull Canadian firms into its supply chain *before* the contract and Value Proposition are finalized, and it's already producing real pre-contract commitments (Valbruna ASW's non-magnetic steel order, June 2026).

## Why it matters

This is the opposite lesson from Lessons 2 and 3, where the risk was misreading a *quiet-looking* ITB balance on an *already-committed* program as "no opportunity." Here the risk runs the other way: a *blank* ITB balance on a *pre-contract* program looks like "nothing to see yet" but is actually the highest-leverage window there is — the industrial team and Value Proposition commitments are still being drafted, not locked. Positioning now means being written into the plan; positioning after end-2027 means competing for whatever's left in an already-assigned supply chain.

The general pattern across all three programs so far: the ISED ITB balance column tells you almost nothing about *urgency* on its own — it has to be read together with which contract phase a program is actually in (pre-contract vs. Definition vs. Construction), not treated as a single comparable number across programs.
