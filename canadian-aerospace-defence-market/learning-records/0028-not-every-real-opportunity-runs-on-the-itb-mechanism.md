# 0028: Not every real Canadian aerospace opportunity runs on the ITB mechanism — some are direct OEM manufacturing

**From:** Lesson 26 (DHC Defence)

## The finding

Every program pursuit in this workspace up to now has been read through the ITB Value Proposition lens — DND buys, a prime is obligated to deliver Canadian-content value, Kanata chases the obligation. De Havilland Canada's DHC Defence division doesn't fit that frame at all: its DHC-515 firefighting aircraft is bought by provincial governments (Alberta, Manitoba, Ontario) and a European Commission-backed multinational consortium, not primarily DND. There's no federal ITB obligation to check. What's real instead is direct airframe OEM production — DHC is actively building a new Calgary-area manufacturing facility, with first-aircraft assembly already underway, and every aircraft built needs wiring regardless of who bought it.

## Why it matters

The workspace's dominant pursuit strategy (find the ITB obligation, find the obligation manager, position against the open balance) doesn't generalize to every real opportunity. An OEM actually manufacturing aircraft in Canada is a direct supply relationship, closer in shape to Team SkyGuardian's "components and manufacturing" fit (Lesson 24) than to any ITB-driven program covered before it. Worth checking, for any newly-surfaced company, which category it actually belongs to before defaulting to the ITB playbook that's worked for most of this workspace.

## Follow-up

No public supplier portal was found for DHC Defence — the honest next step is a direct inquiry to the program development office, not assuming a self-serve process exists the way it does for Team SkyGuardian.
