# 0009: A domain's total dollar figure can badly overstate real fit — sort by what the work actually is, not just where the money sits

**From:** Lesson 10 (Is Land a Fit?)

## The finding

Lesson 9 surfaced ~$2.71B in untied Land-domain ITB money and flagged it as worth checking rather than assuming it's opportunity. Sorted against Kanata's confirmed, primary-source capability description ("electrical-assembly / wire-harness SMB" — found directly in the `kanata-crm-excel-sync` skill's own SKILL.md, not inferred), the domain splits into three genuinely different buckets: ~$1.24B of real vehicle-platform electrical/harness work (GDLS Logistics Vehicle Modernization, Rheinmetall Enhanced Recovery Capability, GM Defense Light Tactical Vehicle, KNDS Leopard 2 sustainment), ~$1.34B of software/systems-integration sustainment that doesn't match the capability at all (General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada's four LC4ISR lines) — and this poor-fit bucket is the *single largest* slice of the domain — plus ~$123M of uncertain, niche weapons-systems electronics (Saab's Soldier Portable Air Defence System).

Also resolved during this check: Kanata's curated-program list is a manually maintained editorial whitelist, not a capability-matched output — so Land's absence was a curation backlog, not a considered rejection. The fit question genuinely hadn't been asked before this lesson.

## Why it matters

A domain-level dollar total is a sizing number, not a targeting number. Naively treating "$2.71B in Land" as opportunity would have pointed a positioning effort at LC4ISR — the biggest number in the domain and the worst capability match — ahead of the vehicle-electrical work that's the actual, defensible fit. The right unit of analysis is always the individual project's real scope against the confirmed capability statement, not the domain's aggregate balance.

## Follow-up

If Land gets added to Kanata's curated list, the three vehicle-electrical programs (GDLS, Rheinmetall, GM Defense) are ready to draft as tier-appropriate rows. LC4ISR should be recorded as explicitly out of scope, not silently omitted, so a future review doesn't re-surface it as an unexamined gap.
