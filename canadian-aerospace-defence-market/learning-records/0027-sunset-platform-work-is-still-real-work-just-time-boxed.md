# 0027: A sunset platform's sustainment contracts are still worth pursuing — just framed by their real horizon, not oversold as growth

**From:** Lesson 25 (CF-18 Avionics & Flight Service)

## The finding

CF-18 is the fleet F-35 is actively replacing (Lesson 12, deliveries 2026–2032+), which makes its avionics sustainment work fundamentally different in character from everything else recently covered — DDIM, Team SkyGuardian, CDIR are all growth-oriented or standing programs. Arcfield Canada's CF-18 Avionics In-Service Support 2 ($211.6M, 2024–2029, Team Northern Sentry) is real, current, and includes a genuine SME success story (Palitronica, a Kitchener-based hardware-assurance company on the team) — but it's revenue with a foreseeable end date tied to the F-35 transition, not a program to build a long-term strategy around.

## Why it matters

Not every real opportunity needs to be ranked against the open-ended growth doors (Lesson 23's "Open Doors, Ranked" reference). A sunset-platform sustainment contract can be worth pursuing on its own honest terms — near/medium-term revenue, a real proof-of-concept for SME inclusion on a major team — without needing to pretend it's more durable than it is. Mislabeling a time-boxed opportunity as a long-term bet would be as much of an error as dismissing it for having an end date at all.
