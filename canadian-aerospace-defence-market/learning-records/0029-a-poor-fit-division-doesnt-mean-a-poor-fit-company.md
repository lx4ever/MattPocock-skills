# 0029: A company assessed as a poor fit can have a different division that's a genuinely good one

**From:** Lesson 27 (General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada)

## The finding

Lesson 10 assessed GDMS-Canada's four Land C4ISR contracts (System of Systems Engineering, Core Network Sustainment, Applications Sustainment, ISTAR) as a poor fit and recommended leaving them out of scope — correctly, they're software/systems-integration work. That verdict was read (including by this workspace, until now) as covering the company generally. It didn't: GDMS-Canada's undersea-warfare division builds real hardware — sonobuoy processors, expendable acoustic countermeasures, and a new hull-mounted sonar (HMS-610, May 2026) — installed on three real platforms (River-class Destroyer, the Maritime Helicopter Project, and Aurora Incremental Modernization), two of which hadn't appeared anywhere else in this workspace. GDMS-Canada is also headquartered in Ottawa — the same city as Kanata — a fact that had never surfaced despite the company being mentioned since Lesson 3.

## Why it matters

This extends Lesson 21's finding (a fit verdict built from partial data doesn't generalize to a whole program family) one level further: it doesn't generalize to a whole *company* either. A large defence contractor can have multiple, genuinely distinct divisions — one a poor fit, another a strong one — and dismissing the company as a whole based on the division actually reviewed would have silently closed off a real, Ottawa-local, three-platform relationship.

## Follow-up

Check whether GDMS-Canada's undersea warfare division has a public supplier-registration process, and consider whether other companies already dismissed at the division level (checked so far: only GDMS-Canada) might have the same split.
