# 0013: Primes subcontract for two independent reasons — legal obligation and operational capacity — and outreach should use both

**From:** Lesson 14 (Global Backdrop / Deloitte 2026 A&D Outlook)

## The finding

Every program-level lesson so far (2–13) grounded Kanata's pursuit strategy in the ITB Value Proposition mechanism — a prime is legally obligated to deliver Canadian-content value. Deloitte's 2026 Aerospace and Defense Industry Outlook (and its midyear update) surfaces a second, independent, industry-wide reason that has nothing to do with Canadian policy: primes across the whole sector are capacity-constrained. Production-critical workforce attrition runs ~15% (more than double the cross-industry average), a third of the workforce is nearing retirement, and specific bottlenecks — certified labor, test cells, tooling, inspection capacity — are named directly. Fitch's assessment (cited by Deloitte) states plainly that fragile sub-tier suppliers are stabilized by "multi-year demand visibility and sustained commitments" — which is, functionally, what an ITB obligation already provides.

## Why it matters

The two rationales reinforce rather than duplicate each other, and a pitch built on only one is weaker than one that uses both. "We can help you meet your Value Proposition" reads as compliance. "We can provide the sustained, multi-year production capacity your workforce and tooling constraints are forcing you to source externally anyway" reads as solving a real operational problem in the primes' own current language. The three outreach drafts written before this lesson (Provencher/MDA, Hurst/Boeing, Hey/Seaspan) only used the first framing.

## Follow-up

Worth revising those three drafts to add a capacity/reliability line grounded in this lesson, and considering it as a standard element of future outreach drafts going forward — not just an ITB-fit pitch.
