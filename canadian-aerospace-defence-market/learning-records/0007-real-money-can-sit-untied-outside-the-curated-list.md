# 0007: A program can have real, sizeable ITB money sitting in Kanata's own workbook while being entirely absent from the curated list Kanata actually navigates by

**From:** Lesson 8 (RPAS / MQ-9B SkyGuardian)

## The finding

RPAS doesn't appear anywhere in Kanata's BD Strategy Map or Canadian Programs 2026+ sheets — no tier, no pipeline entries, no contact. But the ITB Obligations (ISED) sheet, which lists every registered obligation regardless of curation, has a real entry: General Atomics Aeronautical Systems, "Remotely Piloted Aircraft System – Acquisition," USD $496,288,640 still "to be identified." MDA — already established across Lessons 5 and 6 as the hub of Kanata's entire space-domain pursuit — is a paid supplier on this exact program ($74M contract assembling Ground Control Stations in Richmond, BC), and CAE holds a separate, currently-running $250M/9-year training-support contract on it. This isn't a speculative or early-stage program; it has a signed acquisition contract (Dec 2023), a named Canadian industry team (Team SkyGuardian Canada: GA-ASI, CAE, MDA, L3Harris), and money already moving.

This was flagged as a gap during the original workbook review (before this workspace existed) and confirmed directly while building this lesson.

## Why it matters

The curated 21-program list is meant to be the navigable surface of the workbook — but its absence from that list doesn't mean absence of real opportunity, only that nobody has promoted it yet. A workbook's own raw data (the ITB sheet) can contain a fully-formed opportunity that the curated view a BD person actually works from never surfaces. Worth periodically diffing the ITB sheet's contractor list against the curated program list for exactly this kind of gap, rather than assuming the curated list is complete.
