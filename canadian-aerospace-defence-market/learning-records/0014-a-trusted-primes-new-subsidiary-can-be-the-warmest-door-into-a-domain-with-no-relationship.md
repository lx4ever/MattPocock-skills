# 0014: A trusted prime's new subsidiary can be a warmer door into a domain than that domain's established players

**From:** Lesson 15 (49North)

## The finding

Lesson 10 assessed the Land domain's biggest ITB slice (General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada's ~$1.34B in LC4ISR sustainment work) as a poor fit and recommended leaving it explicitly out of scope — correct on the underlying work (systems integration/software, not manufacturing), but that assessment was implicitly working from "the only way into land-domain C4ISR is through GDMS-Canada, cold." In February 2026, MDA Space launched 49North — an Ottawa-based subsidiary built specifically to deliver multi-domain C4ISR (land, air, maritime, joint) outside the space domain, listing "defence-qualified electronics" among its own stated capabilities. Kanata already has a real, warm relationship inside the MDA family (Francois Provencher, Canadarm3, Lesson 6). 49North doesn't change whether Land-domain C4ISR work is a good fit — it changes who the fit question should be asked of.

## Why it matters

A domain gap search (Lesson 9) or fit assessment (Lesson 10) can correctly identify that the *obvious* players in a domain are a poor match, without checking whether a *trusted* player elsewhere has recently expanded into that domain. The right move when a new subsidiary appears from a known-good relationship is to route through it rather than defaulting back to the domain's established (and possibly poorly-fitting) incumbents.

## Follow-up

Worth periodically checking whether other trusted primes (Irving, Seaspan, Boeing, Thales) have launched similar domain-expanding subsidiaries before assuming a gap can only be filled by cold outreach to an unfamiliar company.
