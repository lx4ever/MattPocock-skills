# 0023: A TRL funding map turns "which program should we apply to" into a stage-matching question, not a guessing game

**From:** Lesson 22 (BOREALIS)

## The finding

Lesson 18 covered Strix/INSAT and FedDev's RDII as two separate, unconnected funding leads. Official BOREALIS briefing material shows they're two points on one deliberately organized map: every relevant Canadian innovation-funding program plotted against Technology Readiness Level, from fundamental research (TRL 1) through to market and production (TRL 9+). Kanata — a manufacturing SME building sellable product, not doing early-stage R&D — sits at the TRL 9+ end specifically, where the relevant programs are RDII, Canadian Defence Industry Resilience (CDIR), Accelerated Growth Services, CanExport SMEs, ElevateIP, and Lab-to-Market. Strix/INSAT's Strategic Response Fund spans nearly the whole range, which is why it showed up as relevant even though most of its scope (TRL 1–8) doesn't match Kanata's actual stage.

## Why it matters

Without the TRL map, evaluating a newly-surfaced funding program means asking "is this relevant?" one program at a time, with no way to tell whether a program's scope actually matches Kanata's stage versus just sounding adjacent. With the map, the question becomes mechanical: find Kanata's TRL (9+), read off the programs actually positioned there. CDIR — DND-administered, at exactly that stage — is a concrete new lead this method surfaced that a program-by-program search likely wouldn't have prioritized correctly.

## Follow-up

Investigate CDIR (Canadian Defence Industry Resilience) directly — it's DND-administered rather than a regional development agency program like RDII, which may mean a different application process and eligibility bar.
