# 0011: A blank ITB figure can mean "not yet registered" or "will never be registered" — read which one applies

**From:** Lesson 12 (F-35)

## The finding

Every blank/zero ITB figure covered before this lesson (CPSP, Lesson 4) meant "pre-contract — this will populate once the deal concludes." F-35 is blank for a structurally different reason: under the Joint Strike Fighter program's Memorandum of Understanding, partner nations (Canada included, as a Level 3 partner) agreed not to require traditional industrial offsets at all, in exchange for their industry competing for global F-35 work on a "best value" basis. This program will never generate a registered ITB obligation, regardless of how much time passes or how many contracts get signed — it's not behind schedule, it's a different mechanism entirely. (71 Canadian companies already supply into the program on that competitive basis, ~US$812M in contracts won.)

Kanata's own tracker already encodes this correctly without spelling it out: F-35's contact field says "ITB / Industrial Participation office," not "ITB obligation manager" — the right distinct label, likely because whoever built the curated entry knew the JSF program's actual mechanism even if it wasn't explained in the workbook itself.

## Why it matters

Before reading any program's blank ITB figure as "not yet — check back later" (the CPSP pattern), confirm whether the program actually uses the ITB/Value Proposition mechanism at all. Some programs — particularly multinational partnership programs like the F-35 — are structured from the outset to never produce one, and the pursuit strategy has to be entirely different (direct competitive bidding for global supply chain scope, not registering interest against an eventual Canadian-content obligation).

## Follow-up

Worth checking whether any other program in Kanata's curated list follows a similar multinational best-value model rather than the ITB Value Proposition model, before assuming blank figures elsewhere all mean "pre-contract."
