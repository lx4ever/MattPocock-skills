# 0020: A "poor fit" verdict built from partial data can bundle genuinely different projects together

**From:** Lesson 21 (C4ISR)

## The finding

Lesson 10 assessed General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada's four Land C4ISR contracts (System of Systems Engineering, Core Network Sustainment, Applications Sustainment, ISTAR) as a poor fit for Kanata — correctly, they're software/systems-integration work. But that assessment was built entirely from what showed up untied in the ITB register, which only captured those four contracts. The full Land C4ISR program (tracing to 2017's SSE 42 initiative) includes other named projects — Tactical Communications Modernization, Canadian Forces Land Electronic Warfare Modernization — that are a different character of work: radio/RF/antenna hardware programs almost always carry real cabling and electrical-assembly scope. The original "Land C4ISR is a poor fit" framing, if generalized past the four specific contracts it was built from, would have silently excluded genuinely different, better-fitting projects under the same umbrella label.

## Why it matters

A fit verdict is only as complete as the data it was built from. When a program category (like "Land C4ISR") turns out to have more members than the ones originally reviewed, the right move is to re-examine each new member on its own terms — not assume the original verdict generalizes to the whole category. "Poor fit" applied to four specific contracts, not to everything anyone might call C4ISR.

## Follow-up

Worth identifying who primes Tactical Communications Modernization and Land Electronic Warfare Modernization specifically, and checking whether 49North (Lesson 15) has any role in either before assuming an unfamiliar company is the only entry point.
