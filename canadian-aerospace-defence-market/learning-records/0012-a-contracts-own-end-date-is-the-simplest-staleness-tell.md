# 0012: A contract's own stated end date is the simplest, most reliable staleness check there is

**From:** Lesson 13 (CC-330 Husky)

## The finding

Kanata's tracker cites an "Interim" L3Harris MAS in-service-support contract for CC-330 Husky worth $70.4M, dated 2022-2025. That's a stale note, and unlike every previous staleness catch in this workspace (which needed cross-referencing against news coverage or other sources — the CPSP contact, Lesson 4; the AJISS dollar figure, Lesson 7), this one is visible from the note's own text: its stated end date (2025) has already passed relative to today. The real, current structure — two long-term L3Harris MAS contracts (10-year maintenance, 7-year materiel support, both extendable to 20 years, ~$1.1B combined) plus a separate Airbus D&S OEM support contract, announced March 2026 — was findable in under one search once the date mismatch was the trigger to look.

## Why it matters

Checking whether a cited contract is current doesn't always require deep research — sometimes the tracker record already contains the tell. Any obligation note with an explicit date range should be checked against today's date as a first, cheap pass before doing anything more elaborate; a contract whose own end date has passed is guaranteed to be superseded by something, even before knowing what.

## Follow-up

Worth a quick pass across the rest of Kanata's tracker for any other obligation notes carrying an explicit past end date — this is a fast, mechanical check that doesn't require research per row, only a date comparison.
