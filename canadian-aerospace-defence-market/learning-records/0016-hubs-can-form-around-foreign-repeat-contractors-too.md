# 0016: A "hub" prime doesn't have to be Canadian or ITB-driven — repeat contract wins create the same shape

**From:** Lesson 17 (CAF UAS Landscape)

## The finding

Every hub identified so far (MDA/space, Irving+Seaspan+Thales/naval, General Dynamics+Rheinmetall/land) was a domestic or ITB-registered relationship. CACI — a US company — shows the same concentration pattern in counter-UAS: Phase 1's BEAM 3.0 system ($19M), Phase 2's vehicle-mounted expansion ($169M), and a separate SkyTracker contract ($124M) all went to CACI. But there's no ITB obligation mechanism driving this the way there is with Irving or MDA — CACI is a foreign prime. The accessible Canadian entry point into this specific hub isn't CACI itself, it's the Canadian integration layer underneath it (Cambli's Blackwolf vehicle, DEW Engineering's integration work).

## Why it matters

Hub-spotting shouldn't be limited to ITB-registered domestic primes. A foreign company winning repeat contracts in a capability area is just as real a concentration signal — it just means the entry point is one layer down, through whichever Canadian firm is doing the integration/subcontracting work underneath the foreign prime, rather than through a Value Proposition obligation manager.

## Follow-up

Worth checking whether Cambli or DEW Engineering have any open supplier process of their own, since they're the actual Canadian-accessible layer of this particular hub — not CACI directly.
