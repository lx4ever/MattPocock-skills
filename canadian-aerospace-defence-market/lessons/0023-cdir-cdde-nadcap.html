Lesson 23 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

CDIR, CDDE & NADCAP

Three different ways to strengthen Kanata's position before the next application, not three programs to chase blindly — one is real money with a real caveat, one is a research ecosystem more than a subcontract pipeline, and one is a concrete, priced, timed credential that keeps showing up as a stated requirement.

CDIR: real money, with a caveat worth knowing before applying

The Canadian Defence Industry Resilience (CDIR) program is DND's large-scale sovereign production-capacity financing tool — forgivable loans and contribution agreements, from $25M up to $642M+ per agreement, to Canadian defence manufacturers and supply-chain companies building domestic production capacity. Eligible recipients are Canadian-incorporated corporations or partnerships carrying on business in Canada.

The caveat: funding isn't a rolling application — it's delivered through targeted Calls for Proposals (CFPs) aligned with whatever sovereign production priorities DND has currently identified. CDIR's initial focus is explicitly ammunition and explosives production, including components and materials — consistent with the $1.4B domestic ammunition investment DND announced in March 2026. "Maritime systems" and "dual-use manufacturing" are named as priority sectors too, but they're not the current wave. This is the right stage for Kanata (Lesson 22 placed it at TRL 9+, market and production) but not necessarily the right moment — worth a consultation (CDIR-RIDC@forces.gc.ca) to ask directly whether a future CFP is expected to cover harness/electrical-assembly-relevant categories, rather than applying against the ammunition-focused wave.

Source: DND, Canadian Defence Industry Resilience Program.

CDDE: a research ecosystem, not a subcontract pipeline

Lesson 4 flagged TKMS's Canadian Defence and Dual-Use Innovation Ecosystem (CDDE) as the pre-contract mechanism pulling Canadian firms into CPSP's supply chain. More precisely: it's a 15-year framework linking universities, industry, Indigenous partners, and government to move technology from research to market, with core academic partners so far being UBC, Western University, and Dalhousie — focused on naval superiority, Arctic research and clean technologies, AI/autonomy, and multi-domain operations.

This is closer in character to Strix/INSAT (Lesson 18) than to a normal supplier-registration process: entry appears to run through partner institutions (each university's own tech-transfer or industry-partnerships office) rather than a direct company application to TKMS. For Kanata, the realistic path in isn't "apply to CDDE" — it's identifying whether any of the three partner universities has a maritime/dual-use manufacturing project that could use a harness/electrical-assembly partner, and approaching that project directly.

Source: TKMS Group, CDDE launch announcement; Team 212CD, CDDE page.

NADCAP: priced, timed, and directly on Kanata's process

Lesson 5 flagged the AS9100-vs-NADCAP distinction without pricing it. NADCAP's Electronics commodity scope covers exactly Kanata's work: cable and harness assembly, wire harness assembly, and soldering.

FactorDetail
Upfront cost$15,000–$60,000 per commodity, plus $10,000–$75,000 in typical consultant fees
Ongoing cost$3,000–$5,000/year to maintain active status
Timeline6–18 months of preparation — documentation (2–3 months), internal audits and gap closure (2–4 months), then the formal audit
ProcessRegister on PRI's eANS platform, submit process flow diagrams/QC manuals/CAPA plans, undergo an independent NADCAP-auditor review

The stated payback: 1–2 years, mainly through eliminating repeated individual customer audits and unlocking programs that require it outright — which is precisely the shape of the requirement MDA's Canadarm3 team stated in Lesson 5/6 ("CGP/ITAR, ISO/AS9100, AS9102 FAI, NADCAP-aligned"), and the kind of concrete credential a DDI Technology Questionnaire answer (Lesson 16) would carry real weight coming from.

Source: Glenair, NADCAP Scope of Accreditation — Electronics: Cable and Harness Assemblies; AQM Auditing, Cost & Timeframe for Gaining Nadcap Certification.

How the three actually relate

None of these are pursuits in their own right — they're leverage for the pursuits already covered. NADCAP is the most concrete and actionable of the three: a priced, timed investment that would improve Kanata's credibility on MDA's supply chain, DDI's application, and any AS9100/NADCAP-gated naval work at once. CDDE is worth a light-touch check (which university, which project) rather than active pursuit. CDIR is worth a consultation now, even against the ammunition-focused current wave, specifically to ask when a maritime/dual-use CFP might open.

Primary source to read next PRI, "Getting Started with a Nadcap Accreditation" — the most directly actionable of the three, and the one with a clear, working first step.
