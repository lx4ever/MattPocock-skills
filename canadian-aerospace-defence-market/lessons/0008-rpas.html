RPAS: The Program Missing From Kanata's Own List

This one isn't in Kanata's curated 21 programs at all — no BD Strategy Map row, no Pipeline entries, nothing. It has a real $496M USD open ITB balance sitting untied in the same workbook, and MDA (Kanata's single most useful space-domain relationship) is already a paid supplier on it.

Updated by Lesson 15: the ground-control-station work below now sits inside 49North, MDA Space's Ottawa-based defence subsidiary launched after this lesson was written — not "MDA" generically. 49North's RPAS portfolio has also grown since, and Canada's own name for this platform is now CQ-9B Guardian. See Lesson 15 for the current picture.

The gap in Kanata's own tracker

Search Kanata's BD Strategy Map and Canadian Programs 2026+ sheets for RPAS, MQ-9, or SkyGuardian and you get nothing — it isn't one of the 21 curated programs. But the ITB Obligations (ISED) sheet, which lists every registered obligation regardless of curation, has it: "Remotely Piloted Aircraft System – Acquisition," General Atomics Aeronautical Systems, USD $496,288,640 still "to be identified." That's a real, sizeable, untied balance sitting inside the same workbook that scores every other program in this workspace — it's just never been promoted into the part of the tool Kanata actually navigates by.

Why this one deserves a curated row more than most

Canada's industry team on this program is called Team SkyGuardian Canada: GA-ASI, plus CAE, MDA Ltd., and L3Harris Technologies. Two things make this program a stronger fit than the "missing" status suggests:

  • MDA is already a paid supplier on it. MDA received a $74M contract from GA-ASI to assemble the RPAS Ground Control Stations at its Richmond, BC facility, and separately delivers the combat search-and-rescue radios and designs the Ground Control Centre's information management system. That's the same MDA that Lessons 5 and 6 identified as the hub of Kanata's entire space-domain pursuit — this is a sixth program where that relationship already reaches.
  • CAE holds a real, dated training-support contract — a minimum $250M over the initial nine years — separate from the broader FAcT program covered in Lesson 4's neighbourhood, confirming RPAS has its own distinct, currently-running contract stream, not just a future acquisition line.

Source: GA-ASI, "Team SkyGuardian Canada".

One more reason this program isn't standing still

In May 2026, GA-ASI and Saab completed the first flight of an MQ-9B carrying Saab's LoyalEye airborne-early-warning radar — a modular, wing-mounted sensor pod that converts the drone between ISR, maritime patrol, and early-warning roles without an airframe redesign. This doesn't have a confirmed Canadian order yet, but it signals the platform's integration and payload work is still actively evolving well past the base acquisition contract — exactly the kind of ongoing integration/wiring scope that tends to generate build-to-print opportunity over time, not a one-time delivery event.

Source: The Aviationist.

What a curated entry would actually need

Following the same shape as Kanata's other 21 rows, an RPAS entry would need: Prime (General Atomics Aeronautical Systems), Customer (RCAF / Government of Canada), Team (CAE, MDA Ltd., L3Harris Technologies), Likely Work Packages (ground control station electronics/harnessing via MDA's existing scope, support/test cabling), and the $496.3M USD "to be identified" figure already sitting in the ITB sheet. This is a case where the missing piece isn't research — it's promotion of data the workbook already has.
