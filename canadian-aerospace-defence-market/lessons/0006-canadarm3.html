Lesson 6 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

Canadarm3

Kanata's single highest-scoring, best-documented space opportunity: a named contact, a $916.5M open balance, and a contract history that explains exactly why the ISED register's numbers look the way they do.

What's actually being built

Canadarm3 is Canada's contribution to Gateway, the NASA-led space station in lunar orbit that supports the Artemis program. In 2019, Canada became Gateway's first international partner, committing roughly $2B CAD over 24 years, with Canadarm3 as its offer. The system itself has four parts: a large arm, a smaller dexterous arm, specialized tools for maintenance and science tasks, and a ground segment for command and control.

Source: Canadian Space Agency, "About Canadarm3".

Kanata's own tracker lists Canadarm3's ITB "Contract Award Date" as 2022 and total obligation as $1,319,983,365. Neither number is wrong, but neither is the whole story on its own: 2022 is when Phase B was awarded, not when the program started (2019/2020) or when its largest contract landed (2024). And the $1.32B total isn't one contract — it's the running sum ISED tracks under one registered project ("Gateway External Robotics Systems-Canadarm3") as sequential phase awards land: Phase B's $269M plus Phase C/D's $1B alone already account for roughly $1.27B of it. When an ITB figure looks oddly timed against a program's news coverage, check whether it's tracking one contract or a multi-phase program under a single registered name — this is the same underlying habit Lessons 2 and 3 built, applied to a program with more phases than either of those.

  • Named contact: Francois Provencher, MDA Space (francois.provencher@mda.space)
  • Pipeline status: 1 tracked opportunity, Reach Out, score 21

The suggested next action in Kanata's tracker is specific, not generic: complete MDA Space's supply-chain vendor questionnaire (mda.space/supply-chain) and email Francois Provencher directly, positioning space-rated cable harnesses, power distribution units (PDUs), and test-support equipment against that $916.5M balance. Mapped against the program's actual subsystems from above, that fit is concrete: harnessing inside the large arm and dexterous arm, PDUs for arm power distribution, and wiring/test cabling for the ground segment's command-and-control hardware.

The 200-company supply chain, one program closer

Lesson 5 flagged that MDA's $1B Phase C/D contract alone involves over 200 Canadian companies. Canadarm3 is that same contract — so the "over 200 companies" figure isn't background context here, it's literally describing the supply chain Kanata would be entering through the vendor questionnaire.
