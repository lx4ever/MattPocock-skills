Lesson 1 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

Who Buys, and What's Live

The shape of the market, before any of the detail: three federal bodies decide what gets bought, one policy is the actual reason a prime would ever call a supplier like Kanata Electronics, and eleven programs are the names you need to recognise instantly in a room.

Three bodies, three jobs

A Canadian defence buy is never run by one office. Three federal bodies each hold a different piece of it, and mixing them up in front of a client is a fast way to sound like you don't know the space.

BodyWhat it actually controls
DND
Department of National Defence		The requirement. DND decides what capability the Canadian Armed Forces need and why — the customer, not the buyer.
PSPC
Public Services and Procurement Canada		The contract. Under the Defence Production Act, PSPC has near-exclusive authority to actually acquire defence goods and services on DND's behalf — it runs the competition and signs on the dotted line.
ISED
Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada		The Canadian-content obligation. ISED owns the Industrial and Technological Benefits (ITB) Policy and scores how much economic benefit each bidder's proposal delivers to Canada.

Source: Office of the Procurement Ombud, "Turning Complexity into Capability" — and PSPC's own defence and marine procurement portal.

The mechanism that actually matters to Kanata

On defence and Coast Guard contracts over $100M (outside trade-agreement obligations), the winning prime is contractually required to do business in Canada equal to the value of the contract it won. That's the ITB Policy. Bidders submit a Value Proposition describing exactly how they'll deliver that Canadian benefit, and it's scored alongside price and technical merit — a bidder with a weaker price but a stronger Value Proposition can beat a cheaper competitor.

That single mechanism is why a prime ever goes looking for a Canadian SME subcontractor at all. It isn't goodwill or a warm relationship — it's a scored, contractual obligation the prime has to fulfil, and Tier 2/3 suppliers are how they fulfil it. Knowing this changes the BD question from "does this prime like Canadian suppliers?" to "which of this prime's live or upcoming contracts creates an ITB obligation they haven't yet filled?"

Source: ISED, Industrial and Technological Benefits Policy and the Value Proposition Guide.
