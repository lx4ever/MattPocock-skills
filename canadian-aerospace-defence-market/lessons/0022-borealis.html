BOREALIS

Strix/INSAT (Lesson 18), FedDev's RDII (Lesson 18), and NEXUS (Lesson 20) turn out to be three pieces of one umbrella. From official conference slides — and it also surfaces a second hub aimed almost exactly at the capability Kanata's DDI application should be leading with.

Source note: like Lesson 19, this is built from photographed conference slides with no public URL to cite. Treat it as real primary material, not independently re-verifiable by clicking through the way most of this workspace's sourcing is.

What BOREALIS is

Canada's defence innovation ecosystem is, in its own words, fragmented — good at generating sovereign technology, slow at getting it into operational use. BOREALIS is the innovation accelerator built to fix the second half of that problem: "Accelerate the transition of sovereign Canadian innovations into operational capability for defence and national security while strengthening Canada's industrial base." It sits downstream of three "engines" that actually generate innovation — federal research, industry-developed technology, and other innovation programs — and pushes them through a Research → Transition → Capability cycle. It has three structural pieces: a Joint Program Office (JPO) driving early delivery across government, a network of Defence Innovation Secure Hubs (DISH), and a Team Canada model integrating government, industry, and academia.

Where Kanata sits on the funding map

One slide plots every relevant Canadian innovation-funding program against Technology Readiness Level (TRL), from 1 (fundamental research) to 9+ (market and production). The DISH network is bigger than NEXUS

Lesson 20 covered NEXUS (the Uncrewed Systems DISH, Mirabel QC) as if it were the DISH program. It's one hub in a growing network — each DISH targets a specific capability gap, with real-world testing and builders/end-users working side by side. A second, already-launched hub: the Maritime DISH Pilot, based in Halifax, mission-focused on autonomous underwater systems and maritime sensing — accelerating next-generation maritime surveillance and underwater capabilities specifically.

This lands directly on the recommendation from revisiting the DDI Stream 1 application angle: lead with maritime uncrewed vehicle (USV/UUV) harnessing as Kanata's strongest capability match, given the proven naval electrical-assembly track record (Lessons 3, 7, 11). The Maritime DISH is a second concrete door into exactly that space — not local to Ottawa the way the C4ISR & Beyond conference (Lesson 21) is, but a closer capability match than NEXUS's more general/aerial-leaning scope.

NEXUS's priority areas, more precisely than Lesson 20 had them

The official slide lists NEXUS's priority areas as: uncrewed systems, counter-uncrewed systems, automation and human-machine teaming, contested environments, and sensor integration — broader than Lesson 20's summary ("counter-uncrewed systems and counter-robotics/autonomous systems"), which undersold it the same way Lesson 16 undersold DDI's Stream 1 before Lesson 19 corrected it.
