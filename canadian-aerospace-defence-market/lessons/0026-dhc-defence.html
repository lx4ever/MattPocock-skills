Lesson 26 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

DHC Defence (De Havilland Canada)

A genuinely new prime for this workspace — and a structurally different kind of opportunity from almost everything covered so far: an aircraft OEM actually building airframes in Canada, for provincial and foreign governments, not chasing a federal ITB Value Proposition.

What De Havilland Defence is

De Havilland Defence is the special-mission-aircraft division of De Havilland Aircraft of Canada (DHC), led by Sandra Howell, VP of Program Development and Government Relations. Three programs:

ProgramRole
Twin Otter GuardianSpecial-mission variant of the Twin Otter, built for Arctic resupply through desert special-forces insertion — the most directly "defence" of the three
Special Mission Dash 8Government/military-customized Dash 8 solutions
DHC-515 FirefighterThe world's only purpose-built amphibious firefighting aircraft — public-safety-focused, not defence, but real, current, and the program with the most active production

A fourth line, the Short C-23 Sherpa (acquired 2019), is under consideration for a restart targeting defence and firefighting markets — not yet active.

Source: De Havilland Canada, "De Havilland Defence".

A different kind of customer base than most of this workspace

DHC-515 orders are real and dated: a European Commission-backed joint procurement of 22 aircraft for six countries, plus direct provincial orders — Alberta ordered 5 (~C$400M), with Manitoba and Ontario committed. This is structurally different from almost every program covered in Lessons 2–25: the buyers are provincial and foreign governments and firefighting agencies, not primarily DND running an ITB Value Proposition. There's no federal ITB obligation mechanism to check here the way there was for CMMA or River-class Destroyer — this is closer to direct OEM aircraft manufacturing than a defence-procurement pursuit.

Sources: FlightGlobal, on Alberta's order; De Havilland Canada, March 2026 production update.

Real, active Canadian manufacturing — meaning real harness demand

DHC is actively building out Canadian production capacity: a new facility, De Havilland Field, near Calgary, aiming by 2030 to run parts distribution, parts manufacturing, and final assembly for both the DHC-515 and Twin Otter. Production on the first DHC-515 is already underway there — the cockpit and hull were recently joined on the Calgary aerostructure assembly line. This complements existing parts manufacturing in Victoria, BC (from the Viking Air heritage that revived Twin Otter production) and a new engineering/customer-support centre in Toronto.

Every aircraft assembled needs extensive internal wiring. This is airframe OEM production actually ramping up inside Canada right now — a genuine, direct manufacturing-supply opportunity, closer in shape to Team SkyGuardian's "components and manufacturing" fit (Lesson 24) than to an ITB-obligation chase.

Source: Air Data News, on De Havilland Field.

The gap, honestly

No public supplier-registration portal or procurement contact was found this session — unlike Team SkyGuardian's named application, this doesn't have a clear, documented front door yet. The realistic next step is a direct inquiry to De Havilland Defence's program development office (Sandra Howell's team) rather than assuming a self-serve process exists.
