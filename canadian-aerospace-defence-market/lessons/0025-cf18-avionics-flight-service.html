Lesson 25 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

CF-18 Avionics & Flight Service

Real, current contracts on the fleet F-35 (Lesson 12) is replacing — worth pursuing for what it is: near-term sustainment revenue on a sunset platform, with a genuine SME proof-of-concept already sitting on the team.

The honest frame first

CF-18 is the outgoing fighter fleet. F-35 deliveries run 2026–2032+ (Lesson 12), which puts a real, foreseeable horizon on how long CF-18 sustainment work keeps generating contracts — this is near/medium-term revenue, not a long-term growth bet the way DDIM or Team SkyGuardian (Lessons 16, 24) are. Worth pursuing on those terms, not oversold as more than that.

Avionics: Arcfield Canada and Team Northern Sentry

Arcfield Canada has been the prime contractor for CF-18 avionics in-service support for over three decades, through the Optimized Weapon System Support (OWSS) program — a contract Kanata's own ITB tracker already shows as $480.2M, fully completed. Its successor, CF-18 Avionics In-Service Support 2, was awarded in March 2024: firm 5-year term, up to $211.6M CAD. Scope: maintenance/repair/overhaul, engineering support, cyber materiel assurance, supply chain management, logistics support analysis, and obsolescence management for the full CF-18 avionics suite.

Arcfield assembled Team Northern Sentry to deliver it: Raytheon Canada, L3Harris Technologies, and two smaller Canadian firms — Palitronica and Terra Nova.

Sources: Arcfield, March 2024; Arcfield, Team Northern Sentry announcement.

A real SME already on the team

Palitronica is a Kitchener, Ontario company founded in 2019 — genuinely Kanata-scale, not a multinational. It provides hardware assurance for defence electronics supply chains: detecting counterfeit, tampered, or altered electronics in critical systems, including explicitly auditing fighter-jet electronics. Its presence on Team Northern Sentry is the same kind of proof-of-concept Lesson 3 found with W.R. Davis Engineering on River-class Destroyer, and Lesson 2 found with COTA Aviation on CMMA: a real, small Canadian company that won a seat on a major sustainment team, not just a multinational prime's subcontract line.

Source: The Logic, on Palitronica.

Flight service: Top Aces' adversary air training

Separately, Top Aces (now part of Discovery Air/DADS) holds a long-running Contracted Airborne Training Services agreement with the RCAF — adversary air (ADAIR) and joint terminal attack controller (JTAC) training support, first signed in 2005, most recently amended to run through 2029. This is the "flight service" half: contracted flying support for CF-18 (and broader RCAF) training, distinct from the avionics ISS contract above. It already appeared once in this workspace, untied, in Lesson 9's Training-domain gap list.

Source: Skies Mag, "Aces for hire".

Where this fits Kanata

The avionics ISS contract's stated scope — obsolescence management, repair/overhaul, logistics support — is a plausible fit for cable/harness assembly and replacement-parts work on an aging fleet. The real entry point isn't Arcfield cold; it's the same logic Team Northern Sentry itself demonstrates: a prime building a sustainment team explicitly includes smaller Canadian specialists (Palitronica, Terra Nova) alongside the multinationals. Worth approaching as "who manages Team Northern Sentry's supplier additions" rather than assuming only Raytheon/L3Harris-scale companies are eligible.

Primary source to read next Arcfield Canada, Team Northern Sentry — the team's own page, the most direct place to look for a supplier-engagement contact.
