CAF UAS Initiatives and Capabilities

RPAS (Lessons 8 & 15) is one tier of a three-tier picture. There's also a fast-scaling small-quadcopter program and a whole separate counter-drone project — and DDI's Stream 1 (Lesson 16) is built to span all three at once.

Tactical: the Army's General Purpose UAS program

An enduring program to acquire roughly 1,000 quadcopters, distributed to units for hands-on learning and developing operating concepts — deliberately simple enough that any soldier can operate one after a brief tutorial, no specialized training required. An initial tranche of Teal 2 drones was procured as an Urgent Operational Requirement in 2022; a March 2024 follow-on order added 50 more Teal 2 units for $4M (~$80K/unit). The Army is now running an RFP for additional platforms from multiple suppliers, and 3rd Canadian Division's drone fleet is reported to be scaling up through 2026.

This tier runs on completely different unit economics than everything else in this workspace: many small, frequent buys (tens of thousands of dollars per order) instead of one large multi-billion-dollar program. That's a different BD motion — staying visible for repeat small orders and qualifying quickly — rather than the multi-year ITB Value Proposition chase used on CMMA, River-class Destroyer, or CPSP. It's also a genuinely lower barrier to entry: quadcopter-scale wiring and electrical assemblies are a smaller, faster qualification step than MALE UAS or naval harnessing.

Sources: Canadian Army Today; DND, Western Sentinel, February 2026.

Counter-UAS: a real program, real contracts, and a repeat prime

Phase 1 (2025, $46M total) armed CAF members on Operation REASSURANCE (the NATO battle group in Latvia) against Class 1 (small) hostile drones, via three separate contracts: TRD Systems of Singapore (ORION-H9 dismounted directional system, $2M), CACI Inc. of the US (BEAM 3.0 omni-directional system, $19M), and Leonardo UK (Falcon Shield fixed-site system, $25M).

Phase 2 ($169M, awarded to CACI) expands this onto vehicle-mounted platforms — the baseline vehicle is Cambli's Blackwolf, a Canadian-made 4x4 armoured truck from a 50-year-old family-owned Ontario company, with integration by another Canadian firm, DEW Engineering and Development. First integrated system is expected fall 2026, full operational capability by early 2028.

CACI shows up three times now: Phase 1's BEAM 3.0 ($19M), Phase 2's vehicle-mounted expansion ($169M), and a separate SkyTracker C-UAS contract ($124M, August 2025). That's the same repeat-contractor shape Lessons 5, 9, and 11 found with MDA, General Dynamics, and Irving/Seaspan — except CACI is a US company, not a Canadian prime with an ITB obligation driving the relationship. The accessible Canadian entry points into this specific hub look like Cambli and DEW Engineering — the vehicle-integration layer — rather than CACI itself.

Sources: Canadian Defence Review; DND, February 2024; Canadian Defence Review, on Cambli.

A recurring innovation door: the CUAS Sandbox

DND's IDEaS program runs an annual Counter-Uncrewed Aerial Systems Sandbox at DRDC Suffield, Alberta — a live testing/demonstration event for detect-and-defeat technology against small UAS, with real prize money (up to $1.75M in the 2026 cycle). The 2026 sandbox runs September 14–October 9, with its own prior application deadline already passed; a 2025 "Urban" edition ran before it, suggesting this repeats annually — worth watching for a future cycle's application window rather than assuming this one's timeline applies.

How this connects to Lesson 16

DDI's Stream 1 is explicitly "uncrewed and counter-uncrewed systems and components" — meaning the marketplace covers all three tiers on this page under one pre-qualification process, not just RPAS-scale platforms. A component supplier positioning for DDI should read its own capability across quadcopter-scale, MALE-scale, and counter-UAS-integration work, not assume it only applies to one tier.
