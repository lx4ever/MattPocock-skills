Lesson 3 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

River-class Destroyer

Canada's biggest defence program, and the one where Kanata's tracker already scores highest (27) — but the ISED obligation balance for it reads "$0 to be identified." Those two facts aren't in conflict once you know why.

What's actually being bought

The River-class Destroyer — formerly the Canadian Surface Combatant (CSC) — replaces both the retired Iroquois-class destroyers and the Halifax-class frigates with a single 15-ship fleet: the largest and most complex shipbuilding program Canada has undertaken since the Second World War.

Sources: Janes, Naval News, and DND's River-class Destroyer project page.

Why "$0 to be identified" doesn't mean the ITB opportunity is closed

Kanata's own ITB tracker shows River-class Destroyer's registered obligation at ~$937M, with $417.9M "completed," $519.3M "in progress," and $0 "to be identified." Read on its own, that looks like a program with no open Canadian-content balance left to chase. It isn't — that $937M figure is the 2019 Definition contract, a design/planning-phase award that's now essentially fully committed. The construction contract — $8B CAD for just the first three ships, awarded in March 2025, with 12 more hulls still to come — is a separate, vastly larger award whose own ITB obligation hasn't been broken out on ISED's per-project register the same way yet.

This is exactly why Kanata's Pipeline sheet doesn't agree with the ITB balance column: 29 opportunities, a top score of 27 — the highest of any program covered so far — built from live news signals (subcontract awards, supplier-portal activity), not from the ISED dollar balance. When a program's registered ITB balance and its live pipeline signal disagree this sharply, trust the live signal and treat the ISED balance as lagging, not leading. The same lag showed up differently in Lesson 2 (CMMA's "$0 completed" despite a real, announced $61M investment) — two different symptoms of the same underlying fact: ISED's register is an accounting snapshot, not a live feed.

Proof this already works for a company Kanata's size

In July 2024, Irving Shipbuilding awarded W.R. Davis Engineering — an Ottawa-based engineering firm, not a multinational prime — a contract worth over $30M to design and build the engine intake/exhaust systems and infrared suppression devices for the first three ships. That's a real, dated, real-dollar subcontract landing on exactly the kind of specialized build-to-print scope a Tier 2/3 Canadian supplier competes for, on this exact program, less than a year after the construction contract was signed.

Source: Canadian Defence Review.

The industry team and where Kanata stands

Beyond Irving (prime) and Lockheed Martin Canada (combat systems integrator), Kanata's tracker lists CAE, MDA, L3Harris Technologies, General Dynamics Mission Systems-Canada, Raytheon Canada, Ultra Maritime, Thales Canada, and SEA Canada as part of the implementation-phase team.

Kanata's pipeline already has this program at:

  • 29 tracked opportunities — 26 Review, 2 Reach Out, 1 New, top score 27.
  • Two registered ITB contacts: Aaron Plamondon, Irving Shipbuilding, and Andrew Coates, Lockheed Martin Canada.
  • Suggested next action: register in the Irving Shipbuilding supplier portal and send a CGP/AS9100/IPC-620 capability one-pager to LMC Supplier Management, positioning for harness and electrical build-to-print work ahead of the multi-decade sustainment tail.
