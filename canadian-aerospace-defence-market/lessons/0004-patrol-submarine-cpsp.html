Canadian Patrol Submarine Project (CPSP)

The largest defence procurement in Canadian history, and the earliest-stage program covered so far: no contract signed, no ITB obligation registered anywhere — which is exactly why it's the most time-sensitive entry point Kanata has right now.

What's actually being bought

FactDetail
DesignThyssenKrupp Marine Systems' (TKMS) Type 212CD — the same design being built for Germany and Norway
FleetUp to 12 submarines, replacing the Victoria-class (retiring mid-to-late 2030s)
SelectionTKMS named preferred supplier July 6, 2026, beating Hanwha Ocean's (South Korea) KSS-III bid
Contract statusPreferred supplier only — negotiations underway, contract to conclude no later than end of 2027
First deliveryFirst 4 submarines by 2034, ahead of the original schedule

Sources: PM of Canada, July 6 2026 announcement; Naval News; Defense News.

Two different dollar figures — don't conflate them

You'll see this program's cost quoted anywhere from $70B USD to $100B CAD to $86B, and those aren't inconsistent reporting — they're answering different questions. The $70B USD (~$100B CAD) figure is the full-lifecycle program cost: design, build, new infrastructure, weapons, and decades of maintenance over the life of the boats. The ~$86B figure — the one in Kanata's own tracker — is specifically the industrial package: the portion that becomes Canadian-content obligation. That's the narrower, more relevant number for BD purposes, because it's the figure that actually turns into subcontracts. Always ask which of the two a source is quoting before comparing it to another program's number.

Source: Quwa, "Canada Selects the Type 212CD: What the $86 Billion Industrial Package Actually Involves".

No ITB registered yet — and that's the opportunity, not the obstacle

Kanata's own tracker is explicit about this program: "No ITB obligation on ISED register (contract not yet awarded / not ITB-governed)." Unlike CMMA or River-class Destroyer, where the industrial team and obligation figures are already locked in, CPSP's Value Proposition is still being negotiated. That's a materially different kind of opportunity: getting into TKMS's plan now means being written into the industrial commitment before it's finalized, rather than trying to break into a supply chain that's already been assigned.

Real, pre-contract activity backs this up. TKMS has already stood up the Canadian Defence and Dual-Use Innovation Ecosystem (CDDE) — its mechanism for pulling Canadian research institutions and smaller firms into the CPSP supply chain before the contract is even signed. IBM has already joined through it, contributing hybrid cloud and quantum-computing access. Separately, in June 2026, Valbruna ASW in Welland, Ontario received a real 70-tonne order of non-magnetic submarine steel for material qualification — with TKMS stating the explicit long-term aim of establishing that production inside Canada.

Source: Team 212CD (TKMS's Canadian partnership site) and reporting summarized via Quwa.

The industry team and where Kanata stands

Kanata's tracker names the Canadian members of Team 212CD so far: CAE, Magellan Aerospace, Kongsberg, Seaspan, Marmen, IDCA, and Gastops. (Magellan Aerospace notably also appears on Hanwha's contingency KSS-III team — a reminder that Canadian firms hedge across competing bids until a program is actually decided.)

Kanata's pipeline already has this program at:

  • Suggested next action: register interest against the ~$86B industrial package now, positioning CGP+ITAR harness and support-equipment electrical assemblies, while the Canadian-content plan is still being built — not after it's finalized.
