Joint Support Ship

One hull nearly delivered, one still building, and an in-service-support relationship it shares with a program four lessons back. JSS is where the naval domain's pieces click into place.

Kanata's tracker shows JSS's registered ITB total at $3.235B (award date 2020) — larger than the original $2.4B contract from 2017. That's consistent with the well-documented cost and schedule growth on this program (COVID supply-chain disruption plus first-of-class outfitting problems), not an error: the registered ITB figure reflects the contract's value as later amended, not its original 2017 authorization. Same caution as Lesson 6's Canadarm3 finding — check which point in a program's history a cited dollar figure was taken from.

The same relationship as AOPS — literally

Lesson 7 found the real current AOPS in-service-support contract: Thales's AJISS agreement, up to $800M CAD over an initial 8 years (announced September 2023), with 170+ Canadian suppliers already engaged. That contract doesn't just cover AOPS — the "A" and first "S" are Arctic and offshore patrol ships, but the full name is AOPS & JSS In-Service Support. It's one Thales relationship covering both programs' sustainment tails. Kanata's tracker already notes this connection on both program rows, but treats AOPS (1 opportunity, score 13) and JSS (1 opportunity, score 20) as separate, unconnected pipeline entries. A single Thales conversation realistically serves both.

Naval's own hub-and-spoke pattern

Lesson 5 found MDA concentrated across most of the space domain. Naval turns out to have the same shape, split across two primes instead of one:

  • Irving Shipbuilding (contact: Aaron Plamondon) is the named entry point for both River-class Destroyer and AOPS.
  • Seaspan Vancouver Shipyards (contact: Chad Hey) is the named entry point for both JSS and Multi-Purpose Icebreakers — and NG-PCPV besides.
  • Thales cross-cuts both build primes as the shared in-service-support layer, via AJISS.

Read this way, Kanata's naval pursuit isn't six separate program relationships — it's three: Irving, Seaspan, and Thales. Each one reaches multiple curated programs at once.

Where JSS sits on the build-lifecycle spectrum

Four naval/near-naval programs now give a clean before-and-after picture of how to read a remaining ITB balance against build progress: CPSP is pre-contract (Lesson 4, nothing registered yet, but real pre-contract activity underway); River-class Destroyer is early build (Lesson 3, its registered balance undercounts the real construction-phase opportunity); JSS is late build, one hull nearly delivered and one well underway — its $1.15B "to be identified" balance plausibly still covers real outfitting work on the second hull, with the AJISS sustainment tail just starting to open as the first hull approaches delivery; AOPS is fully delivered (Lesson 7, its balance is almost entirely sustainment). The same dollar figure means something different at each stage — JSS is the clearest midpoint of the four.

Primary source to read next ReadyAyeReady.com's JSS progress tracker — the most current, detailed public account of both hulls' construction status.
