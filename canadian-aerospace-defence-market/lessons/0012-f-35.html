F-35: The Program With No ITB, By Design

Every blank ITB figure covered so far has meant "not yet registered." F-35's is different: it will never register the same way, because the program was built from the start to not use the ITB mechanism at all.

Why there's genuinely nothing to check on the ITB register

Canada joined the Joint Strike Fighter program decades ago as a Level 3 partner, contributing development funding rather than a guaranteed purchase commitment. Under the JSF Memorandum of Understanding, partner nations agreed not to require traditional industrial offsets — what Canada calls ITBs — in exchange for the right to have their industry compete for F-35 supply chain work worldwide on a "best value" basis, not a guaranteed national carve-out. That's a structurally different deal from every other program in this workspace. CPSP's blank ITB figure (Lesson 4) meant "wait, it'll populate once the contract concludes." F-35's blank figure means "this program doesn't use that mechanism, and never will."

Source: NATO Association of Canada, "Canada's Defence Industrialization: Offsets and the F-35".

What real participation looks like instead

The absence of ITB obligations doesn't mean absence of Canadian industry on this program — it means access runs through open, merit-based competition rather than a registered obligation. As of recent reporting, 71 Canadian first- and second-tier companies — including Magellan Aerospace, Héroux-Devtek, Stelia Aerospace, and Honeywell — already supply components into the global F-35 program, having won an estimated US$812 million in contracts on that competitive basis.

One detail in Kanata's own tracker turns out to already reflect this correctly, without spelling it out: the F-35 contact field names Lockheed Martin Aeronautics' "ITB / Industrial Participation office" — not an "ITB obligation manager" the way every other program's contact is labelled. That's the right distinction. "Industrial Participation" is the JSF program's actual mechanism; "ITB obligation manager" is the label for the traditional, registered kind this program doesn't use.

What this means for a pursuit

The playbook here isn't "register interest against an open balance" — there isn't one. It's competing directly for global F-35 supply chain scope on merit, most realistically by approaching Lockheed Martin Aeronautics' Industrial Participation office directly, or by reaching L3Harris MAS for the Canada-specific sustainment/depot footprint, which does operate on a normal, more conventional Canadian-supplier basis even though the wider program doesn't. Kanata's tracker already has both of those framed correctly: 2 tracked opportunities (1 Reach Out, 1 New), top score 21.
