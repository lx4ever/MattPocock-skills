CMMA: The P-8A Poseidon Buy

Your first deep dive on a single program — and it's one where the ITB mechanism from Lesson 1 isn't theoretical. Boeing has already spent real money in Canada under this program's Value Proposition, and Kanata already has a scored pipeline entry open against it.

What's actually being bought

The Canadian Multi-Mission Aircraft (CMMA) project replaces the RCAF's CP-140 Aurora — in service since 1980 and due to retire around 2030 — with the Boeing P-8A Poseidon. Canada selected the P-8A in March 2023 (it was the only aircraft that met every CMMA requirement) and finalized a government-to-government Foreign Military Sales deal for up to 16 aircraft on November 30, 2023: 14 firm, 2 options.

The ITB mechanism, no longer theoretical

Lesson 1 covered the Value Proposition as a scored bid obligation. On CMMA, Boeing committed to 100% ITB — every dollar of the contract's value has to show up as Canadian economic activity. That's not a projection; it's already producing named, dated deliveries:

Real example, not policy text: in August 2024, Boeing announced a $61M ITB investment in British Columbia aerospace under CMMA — $48M to build R&D capacity at Boeing's own Vancouver facility, and $13M into COTA Aviation, an Indigenous-owned company in Parksville, BC, to stand up an aerospace manufacturing training facility. That's the Value Proposition mechanism from Lesson 1, executing in public, with a named recipient and a dollar figure.

Source: Boeing Canada, "Boeing Investments Strengthen British Columbia's Aerospace Industry".

The Canadian industry team already on this program

Boeing's Canadian ITB partner group is called Team Poseidon: CAE, GE Aviation Canada, IMP Aerospace & Defence, KF Aerospace, Honeywell Aerospace Canada, Raytheon Canada, and StandardAero. Boeing frames this on top of an existing base of 81 Canadian suppliers already on the P-8 platform, and 550+ Boeing suppliers across Canada worth roughly $4B CAD/year and 14,000+ jobs.

This list isn't just a press-release claim — it matches, name for name, the "Key Subcontractors / Team" field Kanata's own tracker already has recorded for this program. When a public source and Kanata's internal tracker agree independently, that's a good sign the underlying data is solid enough to act on.

Where this sits in Kanata's actual pipeline right now

This isn't a cold-start pursuit. Kanata's BD tracker (Kanata_Canadian_Programs_Obligations) already carries CMMA with:

  • ITB obligation: USD $4,052,653,229 total — USD $0 marked "completed," USD $2,254,221,808 "in progress," USD $1,798,431,421 still "to be identified."
  • Registered contact: Jeff Hurst, Boeing Defence, Space & Security (jeffrey.e.hurst@boeing.com) — the ISED-registered ITB obligation manager for this project.
  • A scored pipeline entry: status Reach Out, score 22, with the suggested next action being to approach IMP Aerospace and Boeing Defence Canada ISS for retrofit/mod harness and electrical-assembly work — leading with CGP/ITAR and AS9100 credentials to clear the controlled-data barrier.

A genuine nuance, not a contradiction: ISED's register shows "$0 completed" for this program, even though the $61M BC investment above is real, dated, and already announced. Don't read "$0 completed" as "nothing has happened yet" — ISED's completed/in-progress/to-be-identified split is an accounting category, not a live news feed. A prime's own press releases will usually be ahead of what ISED has processed and re-published. Check both before you conclude a program is quiet.

Primary source to read next Boeing Canada's own account of its CMMA ITB investments — it's the clearest place to see the Value Proposition mechanism translated into named companies and dollar figures, which is exactly the muscle you need for reading the next program's obligations.
