Land and Training: Testing the Patterns

Eight lessons built a set of reading habits against Naval, Air, and Space. Stepping back to check them against Land and Training turns up something bigger than another missing program: an entire missing domain.

Lesson 8 found one program (RPAS) missing from an otherwise-populated Air domain. Checking the underlying ITB Obligations sheet against the curated list directly turns up something categorically different: Land isn't thin, it's entirely absent — no tier, no BD Strategy Map row, no Pipeline opportunities, nothing.

That's roughly $2.71B in real, currently-open "to be identified" ITB obligation, spread across six named contractors, with zero Pipeline opportunities, zero BD Strategy Map presence, and zero pipeline score to flag it. This is the same underlying finding as Lesson 8's RPAS gap, but structurally bigger: it isn't that one program row is missing from a populated domain — the domain itself was apparently never brought into scope of the curation effort. Several more Land-domain projects show $0 "to be identified" (fully committed or contracts not yet valued) — General Dynamics Land Systems Canada alone has half a dozen more (Armoured Combat Support Vehicle, LAV III Upgrade, Wheeled LAV Optimized Weapon Systems Phase 2, and others) — so this table is a floor, not the whole picture.

Does the "hub" pattern hold? Yes — a different hub

Lesson 5 found MDA Space concentrated across half of Kanata's space programs. The same shape shows up in Land, just with different names: General Dynamics — via its two related Canadian entities, General Dynamics Land Systems Canada (vehicles) and General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada (the LC4ISR electronics/C4ISR backbone) — appears across the majority of the Land-domain rows above. Rheinmetall Canada is the clear second concentration, spanning Enhanced Recovery Capability, recovery vehicle sustainment, and several $0-balance Land C4ISR items not shown here. If Land ever gets curated properly, these two are where a supplier-registration relationship would do the most good at once — the same logic Lesson 5 applied to MDA.

Does the "maturity vs. balance" pattern hold? Can't be tested here

Lesson 7 showed a large ITB balance can coexist with a low pipeline score once a program is mostly delivered, and that the mismatch is informative if you can see both numbers. Land can't be read that way at all right now — there's no pipeline score to compare against, because there's no curated program to attach one to. That's a more basic failure than a miscalibrated score: the tool has no opinion on Land whatsoever, correct or not.

What about Training?

Training is thin (FAcT only) but not empty the way Land is, and what's missing is smaller: CAE holds untied Operational Training Systems Provider contracts for the C130J ($13.3M) and CH-147 ($37.1M), and Top Aces has a Contracted Airborne Training Services line at $0. Worth curating eventually, but nowhere near Land's scale — Training's gap is "thin," Land's is "absent."
