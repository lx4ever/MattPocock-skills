Lesson 15 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

49North: MDA's Second Hub

In February 2026, MDA Space split its non-space defence work into a new Ottawa-based subsidiary. It updates the RPAS lesson directly, and it may be the warmest door into the Land-domain C4ISR work Lessons 9 and 10 found Kanata had no relationship for.

What 49North is

The stated reason: positioning to capture a share of the funding released by Canada's Defence Industrial Strategy, with 49North explicitly marketed as a "sovereign Canadian provider" for complex sensor integration on maritime and drone platforms.

Update to Lesson 8: it's 49North doing the RPAS ground-systems work, not "MDA" generically

Lesson 8 attributed the $74M RPAS Ground Control Station contract to "MDA" broadly. More precisely: that work sits inside 49North, based at a facility in Richmond, BC, and it's grown since Lesson 8 was written. 49North's RPAS portfolio now includes Certifiable Ground Control Stations, Combat Search and Rescue (CSAR) Radio software, Automated Targeting Classification image processing, and — new as of July 2026 — a $3.7M CAD contract from General Atomics to design, build, integrate and test a Coalition Shared Database for the program. 49North is a named member of Team SkyGuardian Canada alongside GA-ASI, CAE, and L3Harris WESCAM specifically (not just "L3Harris" generically, as Lesson 8 had it). One more naming layer, too: Canada's RPAS platform now carries its own designation, CQ-9B Guardian — a fourth name (after MQ-9B SkyGuardian, RPAS, and now CQ-9B Guardian) for the same aircraft.

Source: 49North / General Atomics, July 2026.

Update to Lessons 9 & 10: a warmer door into Land-domain C4ISR

Lesson 10 sorted the Land domain's ITB money by fit and found the largest slice — General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada's ~$1.34B in LC4ISR sustainment contracts — was a poor match, recommending Kanata leave it out of scope rather than chase an unfamiliar relationship for work that's mostly systems-integration and software anyway. 49North changes the calculus, not the underlying fit assessment: it's a C4ISR integrator built specifically to serve land, air, and maritime defence — the same category of work — but sitting inside a relationship Kanata already has real traction with (Francois Provencher, Canadarm3, Lesson 6). Approaching 49North isn't the same as approaching GDMS-Canada cold; it's extending an existing MDA-family conversation into a domain that conversation didn't previously reach.

MDA's hub role, updated

Lesson 5 found MDA Space concentrated across half of Kanata's curated space programs. That hub hasn't gone away — it's split into two coordinated entities under one parent: MDA Space (satellite/space: Canadarm3, RADARSAT/CHORUS, Telesat Lightspeed) and 49North (multi-domain defence: RPAS ground systems, C4ISR, sensing, land/air/maritime). One relationship with the MDA family now potentially reaches further than Lesson 5 originally mapped.

Why this might matter more than just another contact

49North lists defence-qualified electronics directly among its own stated capabilities. That's not incidental — a C4ISR integrator building sensor systems and secure digital mission hardware needs component-level manufacturing to do it: harnesses, electronic subassemblies, wiring for exactly the kind of platforms 49North is now building. That makes 49North a plausible direct customer for Kanata, not just another prime whose Value Proposition might eventually include Kanata — and it's Ottawa-based, which is a genuine local-presence angle worth leading with.
