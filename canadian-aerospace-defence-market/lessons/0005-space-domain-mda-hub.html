The Space Domain: MDA as the Hub

Why MDA is the relationship that matters most here

MDA Space is the prime, or the named key player, on five of these ten programs: Canadarm3, RADARSAT (plus its CHORUS follow-on constellation), Telesat Lightspeed, SofS2, and as a concept-study contender on RADARSAT Next-Generation. That's a structurally different situation from the naval or air domains, where each program you've covered so far (CMMA, River-class Destroyer, CPSP) has its own separate prime and its own separate supplier-registration process. In space, one relationship — MDA's own supply-chain vendor questionnaire at mda.space/supply-chain — is a door into most of the domain at once.

The number that makes this concrete: MDA Space's June 2024 contract to design and deliver the Canadarm3 flight system — $1B CAD, running to March 2030, delivery to Gateway no earlier than 2029 — explicitly involves over 200 Canadian companies in MDA's supply chain. That's not a marketing estimate; it's the scale of just one of MDA's programs. Getting into that supply chain is the highest-leverage single move in the space domain.

Sources: MDA Space, June 27 2024; SpaceNews.

What actually gates entry: AS9100 vs. NADCAP

Kanata's own tracker already flags Canadarm3 as requiring "CGP/ITAR, ISO/AS9100, AS9102 FAI, NADCAP-aligned" — worth knowing what each of those actually checks:

  • AS9100 is the broad aerospace quality-management-system certification — the baseline that says your organization runs to aerospace standards at all.
  • NADCAP goes deeper, accrediting specific processes or product categories (soldering, welding, NDT, etc.) rather than the organization as a whole. A company can hold AS9100 and still not meet NADCAP's bar for a specific process a program actually needs.
  • AS9102 is First Article Inspection — proving a first production unit conforms to the full drawing package before volume production starts.

NADCAP-accredited suppliers are listed on a public, searchable Qualified Manufacturers List (QML) maintained by PRI. If Kanata already holds any NADCAP accreditation relevant to harness/cable assembly, that's worth leading with in the MDA vendor questionnaire — it's a specific, checkable credential, not just a claim.

Source: Performance Review Institute, "What is a Nadcap Supplier?"

The second, smaller hub: Kepler Communications

Kepler Communications shows up three times in the curated list — prime on Kepler Optical Relay Network (already flying: first 10 satellites launched January 2026), "positioning strongly" on the pre-award ESSP, and a concept-study contender on RADARSAT Next-Generation. Unlike MDA, none of Kepler's programs have a locked prime contract yet outside its own Optical Relay Network — which makes this the same situation Lesson 4 covered on CPSP: a growing relationship with real, dated activity (satellites already launched) but pre-contract territory everywhere else. Positioning with Kepler now, while ESSP is still pre-award, carries the same urgency logic as CPSP's CDDE window.

Where Kanata already stands

Canadarm3 is the one space program with a real, actionable pipeline entry: status Reach Out, score 21, ITB balance $916.5M USD still "to be identified," and a named contact — Francois Provencher at MDA Space. The suggested next action is concrete and already spelled out in Kanata's own tracker: complete the MDA Space supply-chain vendor questionnaire and position space-rated cable harnesses, PDUs, and test-support equipment directly against that balance.
