Why the ITB balance and the pipeline score disagree — and why that's fine

Kanata's tracker shows AOPS at a total ITB obligation of $5.7B, with $2.02B still "to be identified" — a huge open figure. But the same tracker's Pipeline sheet has just one tracked opportunity for this program, scored 13: the lowest of any program covered in this workspace so far. Lesson 3 taught "trust the pipeline signal over a stale ITB balance" — but that's not quite what's happening here. AOPS's RCN fleet is fully delivered. A big open ITB balance on an essentially-built fleet isn't signalling new-build harness work the way it did on River-class Destroyer; it's signalling sustainment and modernization work instead — a different category of opportunity that the automated news-scoring behind the pipeline sheet may simply be tuned to under-rate, since "in-service support" doesn't generate the same volume of announcement-style news as a construction milestone does. The lesson isn't "ignore the ITB number here" — it's "read the type of remaining obligation against the program's actual delivery stage," and treat a low automated score on a mature program as a cue to check manually, not as a verdict.

The real in-service support contract — bigger and more current than Kanata's own note

Kanata's tracker cites "Thales JV - AOPS & JSS In-Service Support ($305M, 2012-2030)." The current, real contract is larger and more recent: Thales's AJISS agreement (AOPS + JSS In-Service Support), announced September 2023, covers an initial 8-year service period worth up to $800M CAD, with an option to extend to 35 years — refits, repairs, maintenance and training for both fleets. Thales already works with more than 170 Canadian suppliers to deliver it, explicitly including SMEs, Indigenous businesses, startups and academia. That $305M/2012-2030 figure in Kanata's tracker looks like an older, superseded registration — worth flagging back the same way Lesson 4 flagged the stale CPSP contact.

Source: Thales Group, "Thales Delivers Innovative In-Service Support for New AOPS Vessel".

Where this actually fits Kanata

Kanata's tracker already names the right kind of scope for this program's current stage: sustainment electrical assemblies, combat system wiring, modernization kits, and in-service repair kits — build-to-print harnessing for the remaining hulls, plus in-service-support electrical work through the Thales JV. Given 170+ suppliers are already inside the AJISS relationship, the entry point here is Thales, not just Irving.

Primary source to read next Thales's own AJISS announcement — the clearest picture of how a mature sustainment contract at this scale actually structures its Canadian supplier base.
