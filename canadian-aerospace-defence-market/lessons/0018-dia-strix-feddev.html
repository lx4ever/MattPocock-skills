Lesson 18 · Canadian Aerospace, Space and Defence Market

DIA, Strix/INSAT & FedDev: Doors Beyond Customer Contracts

Everything so far has been about winning subcontracts from a customer. These three are different in kind: one is who actually runs the buying process now, and two are places Kanata could get funded to build capability, not paid for delivered product.

A correction first

"INSED" doesn't turn up as its own entity anywhere in the record. It's almost certainly INSAT — the Initiative for Sustainable Aviation Technology, Strix's flagship program (see below). Flagging this the same way Lesson 4 corrected a stale contact and Lesson 9/10 corrected an assumption: better to say so plainly than to silently guess and move on.

DIA: this updates Lesson 1's "three bodies"

The Defence Investment Agency (DIA) was established in October 2025 — after Lesson 1 was written, which is why it was never explained even though it's been issuing many of the announcements cited since (the SofS2 contract, CC-330's long-term ISS awards, the CPSP backgrounder). It's a Special Operating Agency currently housed inside PSPC, combining staff from PSPC, DND, and the CAF under a Secretary of State for Defence Procurement and a CEO, with legislation planned to eventually make it a standalone entity. Its mission is exactly what Lesson 1 described as missing: centralizing Canada's scattered procurement authorities into one body, streamlining decisions, cutting bureaucratic delay. Practically, DIA is becoming the operational front door for a growing share of what Lesson 1 attributed to PSPC generally — worth treating DIA's own announcements as the most current source for a program's contracting status, ahead of generic PSPC pages.

Sources: DND, October 2025; CDA Institute analysis.

Strix & INSAT: R&D co-funding, not a customer

Strix is a non-profit, pan-Canadian aerospace-and-defence innovation organization. Its main program, INSAT (Initiative for Sustainable Aviation Technology), has been running since 2023, funded by a $350M Government of Canada Strategic Response Fund investment aimed at the industry's green/sustainable transformation. Strix has now launched multiple funding "waves" and reports a $100M contribution milestone supporting more than $350M in total project value — and explicitly backs dual-use technologies, not just pure-civil aviation research.

This is a fundamentally different relationship than everything else in this workspace: Strix isn't a customer buying a product, it's a co-funder for collaborative R&D projects. If Kanata has a genuine technology or process improvement worth developing (say, standardized MOSA-compliant harness interfaces for the DDI application, Lesson 16), Strix/INSAT is a place to fund the development itself, not just sell the output.

Source: Strix, "About Strix".

FedDev Ontario / RDII: direct grant funding, and Kanata is in the mandate area

The Federal Economic Development Agency for Southern Ontario (FedDev Ontario) runs the Regional Defence Investment Initiative (RDII) — funding to help SMEs and dual-use suppliers expand capabilities, adopt advanced technology, and integrate into defence supply chains. Eligible applicants include for-profit businesses, co-operatives, and Indigenous-owned companies. Sources differ on FedDev Ontario's specific share of the national $357.5–357.7M RDII pool — one cites $90M, another "nearly $200M" — worth confirming the current figure directly with FedDev before treating either number as final.

Kanata is in FedDev's mandate area. FedDev Ontario operates from four offices — Waterloo (HQ), Toronto, Peterborough, and Ottawa — and explicitly names Ottawa as one of its regional strengths in tech and innovation. This isn't a stretch-eligibility case; it's a direct match. RDII funding is capital to build exactly the kind of capability this workspace has been recommending Kanata develop — DDI qualification work, MOSA/ICD documentation, quadcopter-scale tooling — not revenue from a delivered product.

Sources: FedDev Ontario, defence sector funding; RDII eligibility for businesses.

How these three fit together

DIA is process infrastructure — who runs the buy. Strix/INSAT and FedDev/RDII are capital infrastructure — where the money to get ready for the buy could come from. None of the three replace the program-level pursuits covered in Lessons 2–17; they sit underneath them, potentially funding the capability-building this workspace has repeatedly recommended (certifications, documentation, tooling) rather than being pursuits in their own right.

Primary source to read next FedDev Ontario's RDII eligibility page — the most directly actionable of the three, given Kanata's confirmed location in the mandate area.
